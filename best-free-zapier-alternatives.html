Short answer: if you'll self-host, n8n is the best free Zapier alternative — genuinely unlimited workflows and runs for the cost of a ~$5/mo VPS. Why people go looking for a Zapier alternative

Almost always it's one of two things: the free tier's task limit gets hit within days of adding a second or third zap, or a paid plan's task-based billing produces a bill that scales faster than expected once a workflow gets popular. The two real contenders

n8n (self-hosted) — best for unlimited free usage

n8n's self-hosted edition is free, open-source, and has no execution cap — install it on a cheap VPS (I run mine on a $6/mo box) and you get unlimited workflows, unlimited runs, and full control of your data. The tradeoff is setup: you're now the one keeping the server running. In 2026 that's less of a barrier than it sounds — you can describe your desired automation to Claude and get an importable n8n workflow JSON back, which erases most of the historical learning curve. Make — best for a true no-setup free tier

Make's free plan gives 1,000 operations a month with no server to manage — sign up, build, run. For most light personal automation (a handful of workflows running a few times a day) that ceiling holds up better than Zapier's free task allowance for equivalent multi-step scenarios, because Make's per-operation model is slightly more forgiving than Zapier's per-task model at low volume. Other tools worth knowing about

A few narrower options come up in "Zapier alternative" searches: IFTTT is consumer-focused and too limited for business workflows. Pabbly Connect offers a one-time-payment lifetime plan that undercuts everyone on long-term cost but has a smaller integration library and rougher edges in error handling. For most people reading this, the choice really is between n8n and Make — everything else is a niche fit.

What migrating actually involves

There's no one-click import from Zapier to either alternative. The practical method: open each zap, describe its trigger and steps in plain English to Claude, ask for the equivalent n8n workflow (or rebuild manually in Make's visual editor), connect your credentials, and test in parallel before cutting over the original. Simple zaps port in minutes; a whole small-business stack typically moves in a weekend. Start with your highest-task-consuming zap first — that's where the free-tier relief shows up immediately.

Who should switch, and to which

  • Non-technical, a few simple zaps, low volume: honestly, Zapier's paid tier might still be worth the subscription over the setup hassle — but try Make free first, it costs nothing to test.
  • Hitting task limits monthly: Make's free tier first (no setup cost to test), then n8n self-hosted if you outgrow that too.
  • Building AI agents or multi-step pipelines, or selling automation as a service: go straight to n8n — see how to sell AI automation services for why unlimited self-hosted runs matter once you have clients.

FAQ

Is there a true unlimited free automation tool?

Self-hosted n8n is the closest thing — genuinely unlimited workflows and runs, with the "cost" being a small VPS bill and being your own sysadmin.

Can I import my Zapier zaps directly into n8n or Make?

Not directly — there's no import button. You rebuild each automation, though describing it to Claude and asking for n8n workflow JSON speeds this up considerably.

Which free alternative is best for e-commerce automation?

Make's free tier handles light e-commerce automation (order notifications, inventory syncs) well out of the box; for high-volume stores, n8n self-hosted removes the operation ceiling entirely.
