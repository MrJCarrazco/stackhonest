Tool by tool: what the listicles won't tell you

n8n -- the missed-call machine

The workflow that pays for everything else: a missed call on your business line triggers an instant text -- "Sorry I missed you, I'm on a job. Text me what you need and I'll get back to you within the hour." That one automation converts callers who would otherwise dial the next name on Google. Add a second workflow that pings you when a web form comes in and keeps nudging the lead until someone replies, and a third that texts a review link the day after each job closes.

Honest catches: n8n is a builder's tool, not an app store. Expect an evening of setup and some reading -- my free n8n templates cover the lead-capture patterns so you're not starting blank. And the Cloud plan is priced in euros (€20/month billed annually on Starter), which surprises US buyers at checkout. If a terminal window makes you close the laptop, this isn't your tool -- but then you're shopping $97-300/month platforms like GoHighLevel resellers, and I'd try the $6.49 route first.

Hostinger VPS -- the fine print on "$6.49"

The KVM 1 plan (1 vCPU, 4 GB RAM) runs n8n comfortably for a one-truck operation. But read the pricing page the way I did this week: $6.49/month is a promo that requires a 2-year commitment, and it renews at $11.99/month. Budget for the renewal price, not the banner price. Even at $11.99 flat, unlimited automation undercuts every per-task tool -- see the full breakdown in my n8n hosting options comparison.

Systeme.io -- the free booking site

The free plan is genuinely permanent: 2,000 contacts, 3 funnels, 1 course, all features. For a service business that's a home page, a "request service" funnel with a form, and a thank-you page -- done, $0. Upgrade to Startup at $17/month only when you pass 2,000 contacts or need more funnels. Catch: templates lean internet-marketer, so spend an hour making it look like a contractor and not a crypto course.

Kit -- reactivation email

A spring "time for your AC tune-up" email to past customers out-earns any ad you'll run, and Kit's free tier covers you to 10,000 subscribers -- most local businesses will never pay. Catch: when you do upgrade, the advertised Creator price ($33/month at 1,000 subscribers) is the yearly-billed rate ($390 up front); the month-to-month toggle costs more. MailerLite's free tier is only 250 subscribers now, so Kit wins free; full head-to-head in Kit vs MailerLite.

Koala AI -- service-area pages without the agency

$9/month Essentials (15,000 words) is enough to build out a page per service per town over a couple of months. The output needs your photos, your prices, and your local details on top -- publish it raw and it reads like every other AI page in your market. Skip Koala entirely if you serve one town with three services; write those five pages yourself.

The dark horse: your Google Business Profile (free)

No affiliate link here because it's free and it matters more than everything above: most local jobs come from the map pack, not your website. Photos weekly, every review answered, services listed. The n8n review-request workflow feeds exactly this. If your GBP is a ghost town, fix that before buying any tool on this page.
