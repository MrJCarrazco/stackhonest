Calendly Free Plan Limits (2026): One Event Type, and No Automated Reminders

Last updated October 4, 2026 · ~10 min read · by Juan

Disclosure: I am not in an affiliate program with any scheduling tool, so every link on this page is a plain editorial link to the vendor's own pricing page. Other pages on this site do carry affiliate links and say so. Nothing here is sponsored, and I will tell you when a free plan is not worth the hassle.

Short answer: Calendly's free plan gives you one event type, one calendar connection and unlimited one-on-one bookings — and that is genuinely where it stops. It does not include automated meeting reminders, payment collection, or the ability to remove Calendly branding; all three are Standard-tier features at $10/seat/mo billed annually or $12/seat/mo month-to-month. If the one-event-type cap is your problem, Cal.com and TidyCal both give unlimited event types on their free plans. Every figure below was read off each vendor's own pricing page on October 4, 2026.

The premise I had to correct: Calendly Free does not send automated reminders

I started this page from a one-line brief: "Calendly's free tier provides a single event type with unlimited scheduled meetings and email reminders." Two of those three are right. The third is wrong, and it is the one that actually costs people money.

  • Correct: one event type. The Free column on calendly.com/pricing lists exactly "One event type" under Scheduling. One booking template, total.
  • Correct: unlimited meetings. In the full comparison table, the "Unlimited meetings" row is checked on all four tiers including Free, and "One-on-one meetings" reads "Unlimited" on Free. So the free plan does not cap how many bookings land.
  • Wrong: email reminders. The comparison row "Automate meeting reminders — Send reminders automatically before meetings to cut down on no-shows" is a dash on Free and present on Standard, Teams and Enterprise. Confirmation emails when a booking is made are not the same product as an automated reminder before the meeting, and the pricing page only promises the latter from Standard up.

That matters because reminders are the single feature most people assume they are getting. If you picked Calendly Free specifically so clients would get nudged the morning of a call, you picked the wrong plan. I am not going to tell you how many no-shows that causes — nobody publishes an audited number for that, including Calendly, whose own tooltip only says reminders "cut down on no-shows" without quantifying it.

What Calendly actually costs, annual versus monthly

Calendly prices per seat and quotes two different numbers depending on the billing toggle. The page defaults to the annual column, which is how "$10" ends up in most articles when the month-to-month price is $12. Both columns, read October 4 2026:

Two things worth knowing before you compare these against anything else.

First, the site now sells a "Plus" variant of Standard and Teams that bundles Notetaker (meeting recordings, transcripts and recaps) and Callie, an email-scheduling AI assistant marked Beta. Standard Plus is $18/seat/mo annual or $24 month-to-month; Teams Plus is $24 annual or $32 monthly. The headline $10 and $16 prices explicitly do not include Notetaker — the plan cards say "Not included" under the Notetaker heading. Calendly also notes both are English-only right now.

Second, seats are counted by host, not by attendee. The pricing page's seat tooltip says invitees do not require a seat. For a solo operator that is the whole bill: one seat.

What the free plan is actually missing

Reading the comparison table row by row, here is what carries a dash in the Free column and therefore needs at least Standard:

  • Automated meeting reminders and automated scheduling outreach.
  • Multi-person meeting types — group, collective or shared booking pages. Free is one-on-one only.
  • Payments at booking via Stripe or PayPal, plus custom payment links, prepaid session packages and invoicing.
  • Removing Calendly branding from your booking page, and custom confirmation pages or redirects.
  • Integrations beyond the basics: HubSpot, Mailchimp, Zapier, webhooks, Google Analytics and Meta Pixel are all Standard-and-up. Round-robin and Salesforce routing need Teams.
  • A cancellation policy setting, and booking on behalf of teammates.

What Free does include and most people underrate: unlimited bookings, a customizable booking link and page, the mobile app and browser extension, meeting polls and one-off meeting links, availability rules, 24/7 email support, and contact profiles with notes and reminders. That is a real product, not a crippled demo. It is just a one-event-type, one-calendar, no-automation product.

Three free plans that beat Calendly Free on the exact limit you hit

If the one-event-type cap is what drove you to search this, you do not have to pay $120–144/yr to fix it. All three of these publish their numbers; all three were read the same day.

Cal.com — unlimited event types and notifications at $0

cal.com/pricing lists a free-forever individual plan with 1 user, unlimited event types and unlimited calendar connections, email and SMS notifications, 100+ app integrations, Stripe and PayPal payment collection, two-way Salesforce and HubSpot sync, and a one-click Calendly importer. Each of those is a paid feature at Calendly. Cal.com is also open source and self-hostable, which is the honest reason it can give that away.

The paid tiers are Teams at $12 per user/month and Organizations at $28 per user/month, both labelled YEARLY on the page with a "Save 25%" badge and a 14-day trial. I could not find a month-to-month price. The page I fetched renders only the yearly column and contains no monthly toggle, so I am not going to back-calculate what the monthly rate "must" be. If you need the month-to-month figure, get it from checkout before you commit.

What Cal.com's free plan does not give you: team features. Round-robin, collective and managed event types, routing forms, booking analytics, recurring events and removing Cal.com branding are all on Teams. So it is unlimited for one person and nothing for a team — the mirror image of Calendly's shape.

TidyCal — unlimited booking types plus paid bookings, free, with a 1% cut

tidycal.com/pricing's free plan lists unlimited bookings, unlimited booking types, paid bookings through Stripe and PayPal, recurring and package bookings, a booking page and translation, with 1 calendar connection. The catch is printed in the comparison table and the FAQ, not buried: TidyCal takes a 1% platform fee on payments processed through Stripe on Free and both Lifetime plans, on top of Stripe's own processing fees. Pro has no platform fee.

The pricing shape here is unusual and worth understanding: Individual Lifetime is a one-time $29 (listed against $144) and Agency Lifetime a one-time $79 (against $240), both sold through AppSumo rather than on TidyCal's own site. The subscription tier, Pro, is $12/month or $99/year ($8.25/month billed annually, badged −31%). Pro is the only plan with a custom domain, no TidyCal branding, priority support, unlimited calendar connections and no platform fee.

Note what is not on TidyCal Free: custom emails. The comparison table marks "Custom Emails — personalize timing and content of attendee reminders, and confirmation emails" with an × on Free and a check from Individual Lifetime up. So if automated reminder customization is the thing you wanted, free TidyCal is not the fix either.

Zoho Bookings — the one whose free plan explicitly includes reminders

zoho.com/bookings/pricing.html describes a "Forever Free Plan" for one user with online meetings, notification emails and reminders, and two-way calendar sync with Zoho, Google and Microsoft 365/Outlook.com. Reminders are named in the free plan's own bullet list, which is the direct answer to the thing Calendly Free does not do.

Paid is Basic at US$6 per user/month billed annually (US$8 monthly) and Premium at US$9 per user/month billed annually (US$12 monthly), with yearly badged as saving over 25%. Two add-ons are priced separately and are easy to miss: an extra workspace is US$12/month billed annually (Basic is limited to 1 workspace, Premium includes 3), and a bookable resource is US$9/month billed annually. The plan-comparison table on that page is stamped "Last updated: 1st Oct 2026."

The honest trade-off: Zoho Bookings is the least pleasant of the four to set up, and it pulls you toward the rest of the Zoho suite. If you already run Zoho, it is free money. If you do not, you are adopting an ecosystem to save $120/yr.

Who should actually pay Calendly

Three honest cases:

  • You sell one thing and your clients are not technical. Calendly's booking page is the one everybody recognizes, and that familiarity is worth something real at the moment of booking. Free is fine here, because one event type is all you have.
  • You need round-robin across a team. That is Teams, $16/seat annual, $20 monthly. Cal.com Teams at $12/user yearly does the same job cheaper; whether that is worth the migration is a day of your time against $48/seat/yr.
  • You live in Salesforce or HubSpot. Calendly's CRM routing depth is the real product at the Teams tier and it is not a feature-list tie.

And who should not: if you are a solo operator who just needs more than one booking type and a reminder email, you are being asked for $120–144 a year for features two competitors hand out at $0. That is the whole finding.

  • No-show rates with or without reminders. No vendor on this page publishes an audited study, and neither will I. Calendly's own wording is qualitative.
  • Whether Calendly's Free plan has ever included reminders. I can only report what the page said on October 4, 2026. If it changed recently, the vendor did not flag it on the page.
  • Zoho Bookings' free-plan event-type count. The free-plan blurb names one user and the core features but does not state a service or event-type limit, so I am not quoting one.

Verdict

Calendly Free is one event type, one calendar, unlimited bookings, and no automation — a decent single-purpose booking link, not a scheduling system. The upgrade that unlocks what most people assume is free is Standard at $10/seat/mo annual or $12 month-to-month, and that is a fair price for what it adds. It is just not the only price available: Cal.com gives unlimited event types, notifications and payments to one user for $0, and Zoho Bookings names reminders in its free plan outright. Pick Calendly for recognizability and CRM depth, not because the free tier backed you into it.

FAQ

Does the Calendly free plan send automated reminders?

No. On calendly.com/pricing as of October 4 2026, the comparison row "Automate meeting reminders" is a dash in the Free column and present on Standard, Teams and Enterprise. Free does send booking confirmations, but automated pre-meeting reminders start at Standard, which is $10 per seat per month billed annually or $12 month-to-month. This is the single most common wrong claim about Calendly's free tier.

How many event types does Calendly Free give you?

Exactly one. The Free plan card lists "One event type" and "One calendar connection" under Scheduling. Bookings against that single event type are unlimited, and one-on-one meetings are listed as unlimited, but you cannot create a second booking template without upgrading to Standard, which lists unlimited event types and six calendar connections.

What does Calendly cost per month in 2026?

Standard is $10 per seat per month billed annually or $12 month-to-month. Teams is $16 annually or $20 month-to-month. Enterprise starts at $15,000 per year with a 50-seat minimum, USD only. The Plus variants that add Notetaker and the Callie AI assistant are $18 annual / $24 monthly for Standard Plus and $24 / $32 for Teams Plus. Notetaker is not included in the base Standard or Teams price.

Is there a free Calendly alternative with unlimited event types?

Two, both verified October 4 2026. Cal.com's free individual plan lists unlimited event types and unlimited calendar connections, email and SMS notifications, and Stripe/PayPal payments for one user. TidyCal's free plan lists unlimited bookings and unlimited booking types with one calendar connection, but takes a 1% platform fee on payments processed through Stripe. Cal.com's paid Teams tier is $12 per user/month and Organizations $28, both shown as yearly pricing.

Which scheduling tool includes reminders on its free plan?

Zoho Bookings. Its Forever Free Plan bullet list explicitly names "Notification emails and reminders" along with one user, online meetings, and two-way calendar sync with Zoho, Google and Microsoft 365/Outlook.com. Paid tiers are Basic at US$6 per user/month billed annually (US$8 monthly) and Premium at US$9 annually (US$12 monthly). Cal.com's free plan also lists email and SMS notifications.

Do reminders actually reduce no-shows?

Probably, but no vendor on this page publishes an audited number and I am not going to invent one. Calendly's own tooltip says reminders "cut down on no-shows" without quantifying it, and neither Cal.com, TidyCal nor Zoho publishes a measured rate. Treat any specific no-show percentage you see in a scheduling-software article as unsourced unless it links to a study.
