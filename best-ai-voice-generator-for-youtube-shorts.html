Tool by tool

1. ElevenLabs -- the default, and for Shorts the cheap tier is the right tier

ElevenLabs is the quality bar everyone else gets measured against, and Shorts are its cheapest serious use case: you need under a minute of audio per video, so you don't need the expensive plans. Starter at $6/month includes ~30 minutes of generation, instant voice cloning (clone a voice from a short sample -- use your own, not someone else's), and the commercial license. Unused credits roll over for up to two months, capped at 3x your monthly quota -- so a slow month isn't money burned. Creator at $22/month (~121 minutes) is the step up when you add long-form or want professional voice cloning and 192kbps audio output.

Where it breaks: credits are per generation request, not per keeper -- bad takes cost credits too (a limited number of free regenerations exist, flagged before you generate). And if you're producing at audiobook volume rather than Shorts volume, the math changes completely -- I ran those numbers in TTS for audiobooks. For the free tier's exact limits and what the missing commercial license means in practice, see the ElevenLabs free tier review.

2. Murf pay-as-you-go API -- pennies per Short, if you script your pipeline

If your Shorts pipeline is automated -- script generated, voiceover fetched, video assembled -- Murf's API pricing is hard to argue with: $0.01 per 1,000 characters on their Falcon model, $0.03 per 1,000 on the studio-quality Gen 2, with $10 of free API credit every month and purchased credit that never expires. A 900-character Shorts script costs one to three cents. The free monthly $10 alone covers hundreds of Shorts voiceovers on Falcon.

Where it breaks: this is an API, not an app -- no timeline, no editor, you bring the automation. That's a feature if you're wiring it into a workflow tool and a dealbreaker if you wanted to type text into a box. Note this is Murf's API pricing, which I verified today; their Studio subscription plans are a different product with different pricing. Voice-quality-wise, ElevenLabs still wins head-to-head -- the full comparison is in ElevenLabs vs Murf.

3. CapCut's built-in TTS -- the free route everyone actually uses

CapCut's editor ships with free text-to-speech voices, and if you've scrolled Shorts or TikTok you've heard all of them. It costs nothing, lives inside the editor you may already be cutting Shorts in, and has no meter. The trade-offs are real: the voice selection is the same one every other free channel uses, so you sound like the feed instead of standing out -- and you should read CapCut's current commercial terms yourself before building a monetized channel on it, because I'm not going to paraphrase a license I didn't verify line by line this session.

The dark horse: don't buy anything yet

The honest play for a channel that hasn't earned a dollar: write your next ten scripts, generate them on ElevenLabs Free to judge whether the voice upgrade is audible in your content, publish with CapCut TTS meanwhile, and buy Starter the month you flip on monetization -- that's when the commercial license stops being optional. If $6/month is still too much at your volume, cheaper metered options exist and I ranked them with verified pricing in cheaper ElevenLabs alternatives.
