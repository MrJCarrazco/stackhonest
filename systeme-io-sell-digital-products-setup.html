Why Systeme.io Beats Gumroad's Fees for Beginners

Gumroad's pitch is real: no monthly fee, live in ten minutes. But its current published pricing is 10% + $0.50 on every sale through your own links, and a flat 30% when a new customer finds you through the Discover marketplace. Systeme.io's pricing page lists 0% transaction fees on all four tiers, including the permanent free plan — Stripe or PayPal's own processing cut is the only per-sale cost.

That's roughly $91/month back in your pocket at a modest $1,000/month, and the gap widens linearly with revenue. The $0.50 flat component also quietly punishes cheap products: on a $3 Notion template, Gumroad's cut is $0.80 — 27% of the sale.

Now the honest other side, because Gumroad earns that 10% in two specific ways:

  • Gumroad is a merchant of record. Since January 1, 2025 it collects and remits sales tax, VAT, and GST worldwide on your behalf. On Systeme.io, you are the seller of record through your own Stripe/PayPal account — if you start selling meaningful volume into the EU or UK, VAT compliance is your problem (or your accountant's). For a hobby-scale seller this rarely bites; past a few thousand a month it's a real line item of effort.
  • Discover brings buyers. 30% is steep, but it's 30% of sales you might never have gotten. Systeme.io has no equivalent discovery marketplace — traffic is entirely on you.

My take: if you already have any audience at all (a newsletter, a subreddit presence, a YouTube channel), the fee math says Systeme.io, and you get a funnel builder and email list thrown in free — two things Gumroad doesn't do and that compound over time. If you have zero audience and want passive marketplace exposure, Gumroad's 30% Discover sales are better than 100% of nothing. There's no shame in running both while you figure out which channel actually sells.

Setting Up Your Product, Checkout, and Delivery Email

The whole build is about an hour the first time. No credit card is required for the free account.

  1. Create the account and skip the theater. Sign up at systeme.io — free plan, no trial clock. You can launch on the built-in subdomain; the free plan also includes 1 custom domain when you're ready to look legitimate.
  2. Connect payments first. Settings → Payment gateways. Stripe and PayPal are both available on the free plan (so are regional processors like Razorpay, Flutterwave, Mercado Pago, and Xendit). Connect Stripe even if you also want PayPal — Stripe powers the clean card checkout, Apple Pay, and the order-bump/upsell mechanics.
  3. Build the funnel. Funnels → Create → "Sell" goal. The classic 3 steps: sales page (one long page: what it is, who it's for, what's inside, price), order form, thank-you page. The free plan gives you 3 funnels and 15 total steps, so one product funnel barely dents it. The page editor is functional drag-and-drop — closer to "gets the job done" than "beautiful" — start from a template and strip it down rather than building from blank.
  4. Create the product and price. On the order form step, add a price plan: one-time payment for an ebook/template (subscriptions and payment plans also work). This is where you set the actual amount that hits your Stripe.
  5. Upload the file. File storage is unlimited on every plan, including free — upload your PDF/ZIP straight into Systeme.io, no Dropbox duct tape needed.
  6. Wire the delivery email. Two clean options: (a) put the download link on the thank-you page and in an email, or (b) email-only for a cleaner paper trail. Build the email once, then create an automation rule on the order form: trigger = product purchased, action = send the delivery email with the file link. That is exactly what your 1 free automation rule is for — spend it here, nowhere else.
  7. Test with a real card. Buy your own product at full price, confirm the email lands (check spam), then refund yourself in Stripe. Every delivery horror story I've seen traces back to skipping this step.

Automating Delivery and Upsells With Workflows

The free plan gives you 1 automation rule + 1 workflow — singular. That's enough to run one product properly, and knowing the difference keeps you from wasting them:

  • Rules are single if-this-then-that pairs (purchase → send email). Use yours for delivery, as above.
  • Workflows are multi-step visual sequences with delays and branches. A good use of your one free workflow: purchase → wait 2 days → "how's it going?" email → wait 5 days → review ask or cross-sell email. A three-email post-purchase flow measurably outsells a bare receipt, and this is the free plan's quiet superpower — Gumroad has nothing like it without bolting on a separate email tool.

Checkout-level revenue boosters, each capped at 1 on free:

  • Order bump: a checkbox on the order form ("add the companion checklist, +$7"). Converts shockingly well for low-ticket add-ons. You get 1 on free, 10 on Startup.
  • 1-click upsell: a post-purchase offer page between checkout and thank-you, charged to the same card with one click. Save it for a genuinely bigger offer (the $49 bundle after the $12 ebook); a weak upsell just adds friction. Also 1 on free.
  • Coupon code: exactly 1 on free — fine for a single launch discount, annoying the moment you want seasonal codes.

One structural warning: the free plan also includes exactly 1 tag. Spend it on "customer" so you can segment buyers from freebie-seekers in your emails. You won't get a second one without paying, and untagged lists get ugly fast. For automation beyond what Systeme.io can express — syncing buyers to a spreadsheet, Discord invites, webhook anything — that's a job for a workflow tool like the ones in the best AI automation tools guide, and the free n8n templates include ready-made starting points.
