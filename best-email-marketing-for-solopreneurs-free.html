Tool by tool: what free actually gets you

1. Kit -- the biggest free list in the business

Kit's free plan is the outlier here and it isn't close: 10,000 subscribers before you pay a dollar, with unlimited email broadcasts, unlimited landing pages and forms, audience tagging and segmentation, and the ability to sell digital products and paid newsletter subscriptions right off the free tier. For a solopreneur whose whole email operation is "collect subscribers, send a weekly newsletter, occasionally sell something," that's the entire job, free, for longer than most lists ever grow.

Where it breaks: no automations or sequences on free. A welcome sequence, an evergreen sales sequence, anything that sends without you pressing the button -- that's the Creator plan, $33/month billed yearly at 1,000 subscribers (and it scales with list size). That's the real trade: Kit gives you the biggest free audience in exchange for your time every send day. Who should skip it: anyone whose model depends on automated funnels from day one. My full Kit vs MailerLite comparison digs into the paid tiers.

2. Systeme.io -- the free plan that includes automation

Systeme.io's free plan is the one I point service businesses and funnel-first solopreneurs at: 2,000 contacts, unlimited email sends, 1 email campaign, 1 automation rule, 1 workflow, 3 sales funnels, a website, unlimited newsletters, and 0% transaction fees on sales -- permanently free, no card required. It's the only tool here whose free tier covers the collect-nurture-sell loop end to end: form, funnel page, automated email, checkout.

Where it breaks: it's an all-in-one, and the email side shows it -- the editor and deliverability tooling are more basic than a dedicated email platform's, and one automation rule means exactly one trigger-action pair, so anything past "tag them and send the welcome email" pushes you to Startup at $17/month. I tested the free tier as a working funnel in my Systeme.io review -- the honest verdict is it's real, not a demo. Who should skip it: pure newsletter writers with no funnel -- Kit's 10,000-subscriber ceiling serves that model far better than 2,000.

3. MailerLite -- great tool, shrunken free plan

Here's the update that surprised me: MailerLite's free plan is now 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 3 automations, 1 landing page, and 3 signup forms. The 1,000-subscriber free tier that made MailerLite the default budget recommendation for years is not on their current pricing page. At 250 subscribers, the free plan is a trial with no expiry date, not a plan you live on -- most solopreneurs doing anything consistent will pass 250 in a few months.

The redemption is the paid pricing: Comfort at $12/month ($10.80 annual) for 5,000 monthly emails, or Power at $25/month ($22.50 annual) with unlimited sending and every feature unlocked -- still among the cheapest paid email tools going, and the automations you get even on free are genuinely good. Who should skip it: anyone choosing based on the free tier alone. As a cheap paid tool it still competes; as a free tool it just lost the argument to Kit by a factor of 40.

4. GetResponse -- fine platform, wrong page for this article

GetResponse's Starter plan at $19/month ($15.58 billed annually for a 1,000-contact list) gets you unlimited monthly sends, AI content generation, a welcome series, one custom automation workflow, and a landing page builder. That's decent value paid -- unlimited sends at the entry tier is something Kit and MailerLite don't do. But this article is about free, and on today's pricing page GetResponse doesn't show a permanent free plan, so it can't win here. If you're already resigned to paying and want the fuller marketing suite, my GetResponse vs Kit comparison covers that decision properly.
