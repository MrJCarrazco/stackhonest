Clockify Free Plan in 2026: "Unlimited Users" Is No Longer True

Last updated October 9, 2026 · ~9 min read · by StackHonest

Short answer: No. If you searched "Clockify free unlimited users," the brief answer used to be yes -- but Clockify's own live pricing page now caps the Free plan at 5 users, not unlimited. Clockify built its reputation on a genuinely unlimited-seat free tier, and several still-ranking reviews repeat that claim as if it still holds. It does not: clockify.me/pricing's Free card reads "For up to 5 users," the page's own FAQ says you can "invite up to 4 people for free" (you plus 4 = 5 total), and the full feature-comparison table lists "Unlimited users" as a row that Free fails -- its cell says "Limited members" where every paid tier shows a checkmark. Projects, tasks and tracked hours really are still unlimited on Free. Users are not. Every figure below was read off Clockify's and Toggl's own pages on October 9, 2026.

What changed, and what Clockify's own page says today

clockify.me/pricing's Free plan card states plainly: "$0, For up to 5 users." The page's FAQ backs this up in its own words: "You can track as much time as you want and invite up to 4 people for free" -- the owner plus 4 invitees is 5 seats total, matching the card. Scroll to the full feature-comparison table and the row literally labeled "Unlimited users" shows Yes for Basic, Standard, Pro and Enterprise -- and for Free, the cell does not say "No." It says "Limited members", Clockify's own phrase for its own free tier, printed directly across the table from the "unlimited" label the plan used to own outright.

Why so many roundups still say "unlimited users"

Clockify's unlimited-free-seats policy was real for years and is the single biggest reason the tool built a reputation (several reviews cite "4 million users," most of them presumably on Free). Three independent 2026 trackers (trick-tack.com, fastlancer.org, rewardio.com) all describe this same event: a 2026 change that capped the once-unlimited free tier at 5 users, with two of the three dating it to roughly April 2026. That specific month is a third-party claim, not something stated on Clockify's own page -- vendor pricing pages don't publish a changelog -- so this article reports the date as sourced from trackers, not confirmed by Clockify directly. What is directly confirmed on Clockify's own current page is the result: 5 users, not unlimited, full stop. A fourth source found during this research (performancereviewssoftware.com) still advertises "unlimited users" on Free as of its own 2026 dateline -- demonstrating exactly how a stale review keeps circulating a claim the vendor's own page has already retired.

What's still genuinely unlimited on Free

The headcount cap is real, but it is not the whole story. Clockify's Free plan keeps unlimited projects, unlimited clients, unlimited tasks, and no cap on hours tracked -- confirmed on the same comparison table, same row style, with a plain "Yes" for Free next to "Unlimited projects." For a solo freelancer working under the 5-seat ceiling, Free is still one of the most generous time-tracking tiers on the market: no time limit, no credit card, apps on every platform, a Pomodoro timer, idle detection, and 80+ integrations including Asana, Trello, ClickUp and Notion. The cap only bites the moment a sixth person needs a seat.

The Hubstaff surprise: another "free plan" claim that doesn't survive a visit to the vendor's own page

Looking for a time tracker with built-in monitoring and payroll features tends to surface Hubstaff (hubstaff.com/pricing) in the same searches as Clockify and Toggl Track. The one fact this article did confirm directly, in the vendor's own words, is that Hubstaff answers "can I use this for free" with a trial, not a plan.

When Clockify Free still works, and when it doesn't

  1. Solo freelancer or a team of 5 or fewer. Unlimited projects and tracked hours, no credit card, no time limit -- genuinely the best deal of the three tools compared here at that size.
  2. You're about to invite a 6th person. That is the exact moment the free plan stops working, not a gradual throttle -- budget for Basic at $3.99/seat/mo before you hit it.
  3. You bill clients by the hour. Free tracks time but not money -- no billable rates. That alone pushes most freelance consultants to Basic or Standard quickly.
  4. You need private projects. Free shows every project to every member; if you track multiple clients who shouldn't see each other's work, that's a Basic-tier requirement, not a Free-tier option.
  5. You were comparing against an "unlimited users" review. Re-check the date. Anything written before the 2026 change (or not updated since) is describing a plan that no longer exists.

Verdict

The keyword that brought most readers here -- "Clockify free unlimited users" -- describes a plan that existed, and no longer does. Clockify's own pricing page now caps Free at 5 users, with its own comparison table labeling that cell "Limited members" directly across from a row called "Unlimited users" that only paid tiers satisfy. What remains genuinely unlimited on Free is projects, tasks and tracked hours -- still a real, useful tier for a small team, just not the no-limits deal several still-ranking reviews describe. Toggl Track's free cap (3 users) was never unlimited to begin with, and Hubstaff's much-repeated "free plan" turned out, on its own vendor page, to be a 14-day trial wearing a free plan's name in at least two third-party roundups.

FAQ

Does Clockify's free plan still have unlimited users?

No. As of this article's research, clockify.me/pricing caps the Free plan at 5 users ("For up to 5 users" on the plan card; the FAQ says you can "invite up to 4 people for free," which is 5 total with the owner). The comparison table's "Unlimited users" row shows "Limited members" for Free and a checkmark for every paid tier. Clockify's free plan was unlimited-seat for years before this change; several still-circulating reviews have not been updated to reflect it.

What is still unlimited on Clockify's free plan?

Projects, clients, tasks and tracked hours are all unlimited on Free, confirmed on the same comparison table. The cap is specifically on headcount (5 users), not on usage.

How does Clockify's free-user cap compare to Toggl Track?

Toggl Track's free plan caps at 3 users, confirmed on both toggl.com's own pricing FAQ ("Yes, up to 3 users") and docs.toggl.com's plan-comparison page (3-member seat cap). Clockify's 5-user free cap is more generous than Toggl's 3, even after Clockify's 2026 change.

Does Hubstaff really have a free plan?

Hubstaff's own live pricing-page FAQ answers "Can I use Hubstaff for free?" with "Yes. Hubstaff offers a free 14-day trial" -- not a description of a permanent free plan. Two independent 2026 reviews agree there is effectively no standing free tier in practice; a couple of other trackers describe a single-user "Free" plan that one reviewer found exists only in the page's underlying data and is never shown as a card or acknowledged in the FAQ.

What does Clockify's cheapest paid plan cost?

Basic is $3.99/seat/month billed annually ($4.99 billed monthly), confirmed on clockify.me/pricing and matching five independent 2026 trackers. It adds unlimited paid seats, private projects, CSV/Excel export, and bulk editing on top of everything in Free.
