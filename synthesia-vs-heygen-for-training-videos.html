Learning curve / ease of use

Both tools follow the same basic workflow: pick or clone an avatar, paste or write a script, pick a voice and language, render. Synthesia editor leans toward a PowerPoint-style scene builder -- familiar if you have built training decks before, and it supports direct PowerPoint import on every paid tier. HeyGen editor is a bit more geared toward short-form social/marketing video first, with training and L&D as one use case among several, though it works fine for a straightforward talking-head training clip. Neither takes more than an afternoon to get a usable first video out of.

Capability deep-dive: built for training vs built for volume

Synthesia 1-click translation into 80+ languages is still a real time-saver for localizing the same training content across regions, and it still sits behind the Enterprise tier only. But SCORM export is no longer a Synthesia exclusive: HeyGen Business ($149/mo + $20/seat) now ships SCORM export and LMS integrations directly, closing what used to be Synthesia's clearest differentiator for corporate L&D. HeyGen's remaining edge is price and credit generosity at the low end -- Creator and Pro are credit-metered now rather than truly unlimited, but Pro at $49/month (down from $99) still undercuts Synthesia Pro at $89/month while adding 4K export Synthesia does not offer below Enterprise. Where Synthesia still pulls ahead: multi-language translation depth and SSO. Where HeyGen pulls ahead: price at every comparable tier, and SCORM/LMS without a custom quote.

Where each one breaks in production

  • Synthesia: the credit pool is shared across video, dubbing, and surveys -- a team that also uses Synthesia for translation/dubbing burns through the same allotment faster than the minutes-only framing suggests, and there is still no mid-tier "buy more credits without upgrading" option below Enterprise.
  • HeyGen: "unlimited videos" is gone entirely as of October 2026 -- Creator and Pro are now explicitly credit-metered (600 and 1,000 credits/month respectively), so a team that was budgeting around the old unlimited-video claim needs to re-check actual usage against the new credit tiers before committing.

FAQ

Is Synthesia or HeyGen better for corporate training videos?

As of October 2026 this gap is smaller: HeyGen Business ($149/mo + $20/seat) now has SCORM export and LMS integrations, a feature that used to require a custom Synthesia Enterprise quote. Synthesia still has the deeper localization story -- 1-click translation into 80+ languages lives only at its custom Enterprise tier, versus HeyGen 175+ languages on every paid plan without that 1-click multi-target workflow. If you just need SCORM out the door without a custom quote, HeyGen Business is now the cheaper path. For heavy multi-language corporate training, Synthesia Enterprise is still the deeper tool.

Does Synthesia or HeyGen have unlimited video generation?

No -- as of October 2026 neither vendor sells truly unlimited video on its mid tiers. Both switched to a shared credit pool: Synthesia Starter gets 1,250 credits/mo (about 22 minutes total including the free allowance), Pro gets 6,000 credits/mo (about 60 minutes); HeyGen Creator gets 600 credits/mo and Pro gets 1,000 credits/mo, both metered by video length and resolution rather than a flat minute cap. Only Synthesia Enterprise and HeyGen Enterprise (both custom quotes) remove the credit ceiling entirely.

What is the cheapest way to try Synthesia or HeyGen before paying?

Both still have real free tiers. Synthesia free (Basic) plan now gives 10 minutes of video or dubbing per month plus 500 credits and 3 survey responses, watermarked, no card required. HeyGen free plan allows 3 videos per month capped at 1 minute each, also watermarked. Neither free tier removes the watermark -- that requires the first paid tier on both, which is now cheaper on Synthesia (Starter dropped to 19 dollars per month) than HeyGen Creator (still 29 dollars per month).
