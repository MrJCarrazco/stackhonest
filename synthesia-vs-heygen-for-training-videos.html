Synthesia vs HeyGen for Training Videos (2026): Which AI Avatar Tool Is Worth It

Short answer: both charge $29/month to get past the watermark, but they meter you differently. Synthesia caps you by minutes of video per month (10 min on Starter, 30 min on Creator at $89/mo) -- and only its custom Enterprise tier adds SCORM export and 1-click translation to 80+ languages, the two features that matter most for a real corporate LMS pipeline. HeyGen Creator and Pro plans instead give unlimited video generation at 1080p, with premium features like Avatar IV metered separately through a credit pool. If you are building an actual training-and-LMS program at a company, Synthesia enterprise features are more mature. If you are one person or a small team cranking out unlimited short training clips on a budget, HeyGen is cheaper to start and does not cap you by the minute.

The 30-second answer

Get Synthesia if you need SCORM export for an LMS, 1-click translation into 80+ languages, or SSO -- those live only at the custom Enterprise tier, but the whole product is built around that corporate-training use case. Get HeyGen if you want unlimited video generation without a monthly minute cap, starting at the same $29/month as Synthesia entry tier.

Pricing: verified against both vendor pages, September 2026

Pro is $99/month ($79/mo annually) for unlimited videos with more Premium credits and multiple custom avatars. Business starts at $149/month plus $20/seat/month for unlimited 4K video, more credits, and 5 custom avatars. Enterprise is custom. The Premium-credit pool (used for features like Avatar IV) is metered separately from the "unlimited videos" headline number -- a team that goes 10 minutes over its Avatar IV credit allotment can add $30-45/month in overages even on a plan marketed as unlimited.

Learning curve / ease of use

Both tools follow the same basic workflow: pick or clone an avatar, paste or write a script, pick a voice and language, render. Synthesia editor leans toward a PowerPoint-style scene builder -- familiar if you have built training decks before, and it supports direct PowerPoint import on every paid tier. HeyGen editor is a bit more geared toward short-form social/marketing video first, with training and L&D as one use case among several, though it works fine for a straightforward talking-head training clip. Neither takes more than an afternoon to get a usable first video out of.

Capability deep-dive: built for training vs built for volume

Synthesia whole positioning is corporate training and L&D: SCORM export means a finished video can be dropped directly into an LMS as a trackable course module, and 1-click translation into 80+ languages is a real time-saver if localizing the same training content for multiple regions -- both sit behind the Enterprise tier only. HeyGen strength is volume and speed for a lower entry price: unlimited video generation on Creator and Pro means a training team pumping out lots of short update videos will not hit a minute wall the way they would on Synthesia Starter or Creator. Where Synthesia pulls ahead: LMS integration, translation depth, and being the category training-video standard. Where HeyGen pulls ahead: no minute cap on the two solo/small-team tiers, and a genuinely usable Pro tier at $99/month for a single power user who does not need Business-tier seats.

Where each one breaks in production

  • Synthesia: the 10-minute (Starter) or 30-minute (Creator) monthly cap is the real constraint -- a team producing more than a handful of training modules a month will blow through it fast and face a jump straight to custom Enterprise pricing, since there is no mid-tier "more minutes" option.
  • HeyGen: "unlimited videos" does not mean unlimited everything -- Avatar IV and other premium features draw from a separate Premium-credit pool, and overrunning that pool adds real dollars ($30-45/month per 10 minutes over) on top of the subscription even on a plan marketed as unlimited.

FAQ

Is Synthesia or HeyGen better for corporate training videos?

Synthesia is the more mature pick specifically for training and L&D: its Enterprise tier has SCORM export (so finished videos drop straight into an LMS) and 1-click translation into 80+ languages, both of which HeyGen does not match feature-for-feature. HeyGen is cheaper at the entry tier and unlimited-video Creator/Pro plans work well for solo creators or smaller training teams who do not need SCORM or SSO. If building a real corporate training pipeline with an LMS, Synthesia Enterprise is the safer long-term bet; if one person making unlimited short training clips on a budget, HeyGen Creator or Pro is cheaper to start.

Does Synthesia or HeyGen have unlimited video generation?

HeyGen Creator, Pro, and Business plans all include unlimited video generation at their respective resolutions, with certain premium features (like Avatar IV) metered separately by a credit pool. Synthesia caps every paid tier by minutes per month instead -- Starter gets 10 minutes per month, Creator gets 30 minutes per month, and only Enterprise removes the cap entirely. For high-volume short-form training clips, HeyGen unlimited-video model is usually cheaper; for a handful of longer, polished modules, Synthesia minute allotment can work out fine.

What is the cheapest way to try Synthesia or HeyGen before paying?

Both have real free tiers. Synthesia free plan gives 3 minutes per month with a watermark and 9 stock avatars -- enough to test voice and avatar quality but not to ship anything client-facing. HeyGen free plan allows 3 videos per month at 720p with a watermark, also enough for a quality check. Neither free tier removes the watermark or unlocks logo removal -- that requires the first paid tier on both (Synthesia Starter 29 dollars per month, HeyGen Creator 29 dollars per month).
