Notion Free Plan in 2026: The 1,000-Block Limit Its Own Pricing Page Doesn't Mention

Last updated October 9, 2026 · ~10 min read · by StackHonest

Disclosure: the Notion link marked below sits in an affiliate slot on this site, but no Notion affiliate code is live here yet — and as explained further down, Notion's own affiliate program currently isn't even accepting new applicants, so this link earns exactly $0 today and cannot earn more until that changes. Confluence and ClickUp have no affiliate relationship with this site at all; every link to them is a plain editorial citation to the page the numbers were read from. Nothing here is sponsored, no vendor saw this before it went live, and this site has collected no affiliate revenue to date.

Short answer: Notion's Free plan is genuinely unlimited in blocks — but only for a single-member workspace. The moment a second person joins, the whole workspace is capped at 1,000 lifetime blocks, confirmed directly against three of Notion's help-center pages. What's strange is where that number lives: Notion's current live pricing page, read October 9, 2026, describes the Free row for pages & blocks as "Unlimited for individuals, limited for 2+ members" — the word limited, no digit attached. The 1,000 figure never appears on the marketing page at all. It only shows up in the support documentation, three separate pages, all agreeing with each other and with a fourth independent source describing a September 2026 change that extended the same cap to the API.

What Notion's pricing page actually says, read directly

app.notion.com/pricing and notion.com/pricing (both live, both read the same day) list four tiers: Free $0 per member/month, Plus $10 per member/month, Business $20 per member/month (marked "Recommended"), and Enterprise at custom pricing. The feature-comparison table's first content row, "Pages & blocks," reads straight across as: Free — "Unlimited for individuals, limited for 2+ members"; Plus, Business and Enterprise — all "Unlimited." No cell in that row, on either live mirror checked, prints a number for what "limited" means.

The real number, confirmed only in Notion's own help center

Three separate Notion help-center articles (Understanding block usage, Who's who in a workspace, and Downgrading your Notion plan) all state the same figure in nearly identical language: a Free workspace with one workspace owner gets unlimited blocks; a Free workspace with two or more workspace owners is capped at 1,000 blocks total for the whole workspace, not per person. People added to a Free workspace can only join as owners (not plain "members" in the usual sense), so "2+ workspace owners" and "2+ members" describe the same population. Hitting the cap doesn't lock the workspace: existing content stays readable and editable, you just can't add new blocks until you upgrade or get back down to one person. Notion grants a three-day grace period after you cross 1,000 before restrictions actually start, and — stated explicitly in the help docs — deleting blocks does not restore capacity. Once a workspace has created 1,000 blocks, deleting ten of them does not give you ten back.

If you want unlimited blocks and don't want to pay, Notion's own documentation names the workaround directly: keep the workspace to one person and add everyone else as a guest instead of a member. Guests don't count toward the member-count trigger, though they're limited to 10 on Free (see below) and work "in a more limited capacity" than full members.

The September 2026 twist: the cap now reaches into the API, and it changes how n8n connects

This is new, and it's specific to anyone running Notion through automation rather than the app. Notion's own API changelog states that, starting September 1, 2026, "Free workspace block limits now apply to the REST API" for internal integrations and workspace-restricted OAuth connections — explicitly framed as enforcement of an existing limit, not a new one. A separate Notion API reference page on request limits, read during this research, describes the same change taking effect September 8, 2026. Notion's own two properties disagree with each other on the date by a week; this article reports both rather than picking one, since nothing about the underlying limit changes either way.

What matters practically: the block limit used to be something a human hit by typing too much. Now it's something an automation can hit silently, mid-run, through the API — and which connection method you use determines whether you're exposed:

Notion's reference doc spells out the boundary itself: the limit "applies to internal connections and OAuth connections restricted to selected workspaces. It does not apply to personal access tokens or OAuth connections that can be installed in any workspace." A blocked write returns a 403 with a structured additional_data.block_limit field in the response body — worth building error-handling around if you're the kind of reader who already built the n8n error-handling and retry workflow covered elsewhere on this site, since "Notion free-tier block cap" is exactly the kind of silent failure that workflow is built to catch.

Notion's own pricing page disagrees with itself on guest limits

A second, smaller contradiction turned up while reading three live Notion pricing-page mirrors side by side on the same day. app.notion.com/pricing and notion.com/pricing both state the external guest limit as: Free 10, Plus "Unlimited guests," Business "Unlimited guests," Enterprise "Unlimited guests." But notion.so/pricing — also live the same day, same company, different URL — states Free 10, Plus 100, Business 250, Enterprise "Starting at 250." That is not a rounding difference; it's "unlimited" against a specific number, on two pages of the same product. Neither is flagged on either page as outdated. This article reports both readings rather than guessing which mirror is stale.

Notion's version is the stranger of the two: the cap doesn't exist until a second person shows up, then it applies to content that was already there.

When the Free workspace actually works, and when it doesn't

  1. Solo use, any volume. One member, unlimited blocks, no credit card. This is the case the 1,000-block cap never touches, and it's most of what Notion was built for originally.
  2. You're about to invite a second person. That's the exact trigger, not a gradual approach — the cap activates the moment workspace membership becomes 2+, whatever the existing block count already was.
  3. You're close to 1,000 blocks with multiple members already. Check Settings → People for your owner count today, not after you hit the wall; the 3-day grace period is short, and deleting old content will not buy you room.
  4. You automate Notion with n8n using the default credential. You are now inside the API-enforced version of the same 1,000-block cap if the workspace has 2+ members — worth checking before a write-heavy workflow fails mid-run with a 403.
  5. You need true unlimited collaboration at $0. None of the three tools compared here give you that past a certain headcount or content volume. ClickUp's unlimited-member claim holds structurally but hits storage; Confluence caps users outright at 10; Notion's is the one that looks unlimited until exactly the moment it isn't.

Verdict

"Notion free plan block limit" turns out to be a real limit with a specific, confirmable number — 1,000 lifetime blocks — but Notion's own marketing page doesn't print that number anywhere; it only tells you the shape of the restriction and sends you to the help center for the figure. The limit is also newly consequential for anyone automating Notion: a September 2026 change means the same 1,000-block ceiling can now fail an n8n workflow through the API, not just a human typing in the app. Compared against ClickUp (unlimited members, capped storage) and Confluence (capped at 10 users outright), Notion's free tier is the one where the real constraint is easiest to miss until you've already built on top of it.

More free-tier reality checks from the same series: ClickUp's free plan found a similar "unlimited headcount, limited elsewhere" shape; Clockify's free plan found the opposite — a headcount cap added where there used to be none. Plausible's pricing breakdown found the same kind of vendor-affiliate-program contradiction as the one documented here. FAQ

What is Notion's free plan block limit?

A single-member Free workspace has unlimited blocks, confirmed on Notion's own help center. The moment a second member joins, the whole workspace is capped at 1,000 lifetime blocks total, not per person. Deleting blocks does not free up capacity — Notion's own API reference states this explicitly: "Deleting blocks does not restore capacity." A 3-day grace period applies once you hit the cap before restrictions begin.

Why doesn't Notion's pricing page show the 1,000 number?

Notion's live pricing-page comparison table lists the Free row for Pages & blocks as "Unlimited for individuals, limited for 2+ members" — no number. The actual 1,000-block figure only appears on three separate Notion help-center pages, all of which agree with each other. The marketing page describes the shape of the limit; the support docs are where the number actually lives.

Does Notion's free-plan block limit affect n8n or Zapier automations?

As of a September 2026 change documented in Notion's own API changelog and reference docs, the 1,000-block limit is now enforced against the REST API for internal integrations and workspace-restricted OAuth connections — which is how n8n's default Notion credential (an Internal Integration Secret) connects. Zapier's documented setup uses a public OAuth app with no token field, which by Notion's own description of scope sits outside the limit. Make has two options: its internal-token connection is affected, its "Notion Public" OAuth connection is not.

Is Notion's own pricing page internally consistent on guest limits?

No. Two live Notion pricing-page mirrors read during this research disagree: app.notion.com/pricing and notion.com/pricing both state Plus, Business and Enterprise all get "Unlimited guests," while notion.so/pricing — also live the same day — states Plus gets 100 guests, Business 250, and Enterprise "Starting at 250." Free is 10 guests on every version. This article reports both readings rather than picking one.

Can I still join the Notion affiliate program?

Not currently. Notion's own official affiliate page (notion.com/affiliates) displays the notice "Program is currently not accepting new affiliates" as read in this research. Three independent third-party affiliate directories corroborate the closure and also flag that the widely repeated "50% recurring" commission figure is wrong — Notion's own page states up to $50 per activated signup plus 20% of year-one revenue, not a recurring rate. The plain Notion signup link above reflects that: a placeholder slot with no live code, because there is currently nothing to apply for.
