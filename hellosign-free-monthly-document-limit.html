HelloSign Free Plan (2026): 3 Signature Requests a Month, on a Clock Nobody Mentions

Last updated October 6, 2026 · ~13 min read · by StackHonest

Disclosure: the Zoho Sign link marked below sits in an affiliate slot on this site, but no Zoho affiliate code is live here yet, so that link earns exactly $0 today — it is a placeholder, not a payday. Dropbox Sign, SignWell and Docusign have no affiliate relationship with this site at all, so every link to them is a plain editorial citation to the page I read the numbers off. Nothing here is sponsored, no vendor saw this before it went live, and this site has collected no affiliate revenue to date.

Short answer: there is no HelloSign any more — the product has been Dropbox Sign since Dropbox rebranded it, and it lives at sign.dropbox.com. Its free plan does exist, and it is 3 signature requests per month. But the number is the easy part. The number in the brief is right and the clock in the brief is wrong: Dropbox Sign's help centre states the three requests "renew every 30 days from the day you sign up" and explicitly "do not refresh based on the calendar month", so if you burn all three on the 20th you are stuck until the 20th of next month, not the 1st. Two more things nobody quotes: Edit & Resend on an already-sent document deducts another document from your balance, and the free plan is not on the pricing page at all — the paid grid starts at Essentials $15/user/month billed yearly ($180/year) or $20 month-to-month, with Standard $25/user/month yearly or $30 monthly and Premium quote-only. Every figure below was read off the vendors' own pages on October 6, 2026.

First correction: the brief is shopping for a product that no longer has that name

This page started from a one-line brief: "HelloSign's free tier gives three signature requests each month with unlimited signers and a basic audit trail." Before any of that can be checked, the name has to be dealt with. sign.dropbox.com/products/dropbox-sign/pricing calls the product Dropbox Sign throughout; the word HelloSign does not appear on the plan cards, the feature table or the pricing FAQ. The old hellosign.com addresses now land on Dropbox-branded pages.

That matters for more than trivia. If you are comparing quotes or reading a years-old roundup, "HelloSign free: 3 docs" is the same product under a different badge — but the plan around it has moved, and the single biggest move is that the free plan has been taken off the pricing page. More on that below, because it is the reason most write-ups of this plan are stale.

The free plan: 3 signature requests, and the rolling 30-day clock

The free plan's terms are published, just not where you would look for them. Dropbox Sign's help centre article Can I use Dropbox Sign for free? (help.dropbox.com/plans/dropbox-sign-free-plan, stamped "Updated Dec 04, 2025") says it in one line:

What the page saysVerbatim
The allowance"Dropbox Sign has a limited Free plan that lets you send 3 free signature requests per month."
The clock"Your 3 free signature requests renew every 30 days from the day you sign up."
Self-signing"Note: Self-signing documents doesn't count towards your 3 free signature requests per month. The Free plan offers unlimited self-signing."
What counts as one request"A signature request refers to the entire transaction of preparing and sending one or more documents to be electronically signed by one or more signers... As long as you send the document(s) to the signer(s) in a single bundle, it counts as only one signature request."
Audit trail"Yes. When your signature request is completed, you'll receive a PDF of the signed document(s) plus an audit trail." Self-signed "Just Me" documents "don't include an audit trail since you're the only signer."

A second article, Dropbox Sign free document limits (help.dropbox.com/plans/out-of-free-documents-dropbox-sign, same December 2025 stamp), spells the clock out with an example and adds the detail that costs people a document without warning:

  • "Those documents renew every 30 days from the day you originally sign up. They do not refresh based on the calendar month."
  • "For instance, if you signed up for Dropbox Sign on January 17th, your pages will renew every 17th of the month." Use all three on the 17th, 20th and 21st and "you will still be out of docs until they renew on February 17th."
  • "Making any changes to your document once it has been sent (such as using the Edit & Resend feature) will deduct another document from your available balance."

Read those together and the practical allowance on a free plan is not three sends a month. It is three sends per rolling 30-day window, minus one for every correction you have to resend. One wrong signer email address on a contract and your month's third document is gone.

Dropbox Sign's own two help pages disagree about whether self-signing counts

Here is the part I cannot resolve for you, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Dropbox Sign's own two help pages disagree about whether self-signing counts against the free allowance, and both carry the same "Updated Dec 04, 2025" stamp:

Help centre articleWhat it says about self-signing on Free
Can I use Dropbox Sign for free?"Self-signing documents doesn't count towards your 3 free signature requests per month. The Free plan offers unlimited self-signing."
Dropbox Sign document pricing options"The free version of Dropbox Sign includes 3 document transactions (self-signed documents or signature requests) per month."

Those are not two framings of one rule. One says self-signing is free and uncapped; the other says a self-signed document consumes one of your three transactions. A third article, on the Dropbox integration, uses the "three document transactions per month" wording as well. I have quoted all of them rather than picking the generous one, because the generous one is the one every blog post repeats and it may be the wrong one. If your use case is signing your own documents at volume on the free plan, test it on a throwaway month before you depend on it.

The same document pricing options article also closes off the obvious middle option: "We don't yet offer a pay-per-doc option because we offer the free version of Dropbox Sign for low volume users." So there is no way to buy a fourth document. The step above three requests is a subscription.

What the brief got wrong about signers and the audit trail

  • "Three signature requests each month" — right number, wrong clock. Correct as a count, wrong as a cadence: it is a rolling 30-day window anchored to your signup date, and Edit & Resend eats into it.
  • "Unlimited signers" — not published anywhere I could read. What is published is better framed than the brief managed: one signature request can carry one or more documents to one or more signers and still count as one request, provided it goes out as a single bundle. That is a genuinely useful property — a three-party agreement costs you one of your three. But no signer cap is printed on either the pricing page or the help articles, and absence of a published cap is not the same thing as "unlimited", so you will not find the word on this page. Bulk send — the CSV-driven many-signers feature people usually mean — is explicitly "available on our Standard and Premium plans", i.e. not free.
  • "A basic audit trail" — understated in one direction, overstated in the other. The help centre's answer to "Is the audit trail free too?" is a flat "Yes": a completed signature request returns a PDF of the signed documents plus an audit trail, with no "basic" qualifier anywhere. But the same answer carves out self-signed documents: sign something with the "Just Me" option and there is no audit trail at all, "since you're the only signer." So the audit trail is full-fat where it exists and absent exactly where the free plan is most generous.

The free plan is missing from the pricing page — and the help centre links you to it anyway

This is the finding that explains why so much published advice about this plan is wrong. The Can I use Dropbox Sign for free? article ends by telling you to "Check out the Dropbox Sign Plans & Pricing to see everything our Free plan includes and to compare it with our paid plans."

I did. There is no Free column. The pricing page shows three plan cards — Essentials, Standard, Premium — under the headline "Unlimited signature requests with no hidden fees", and a feature comparison table with exactly those three columns. The free plan's entire presence on that page is one line of small print near the bottom: "Just need to sign a document? Try our free plan", linking straight into the signup flow rather than to any description of what the plan contains.

So the vendor's own support article points at a page that no longer documents the thing it is describing. Nothing on the pricing page tells you about the three-request allowance, the 30-day rolling clock, the Edit & Resend deduction or the self-signing question. If you want the free plan's terms you have to go to the help centre and read three articles, two of which contradict each other.

The cost of the fourth document

Plain multiplication on the published rates, nothing modelled:

  • Free gives 3 requests per 30 days = 36 a year, at $0.
  • Essentials is $180/year for unlimited requests. If you send exactly 4 a month, each one costs $3.75. At 10 a month it is $1.50. At 50 a month it is 30 cents.
  • So the free-to-paid decision is not about volume in the abstract. It is about whether you ever need a fourth send inside one 30-day window, because there is no pay-per-document option to bridge it.

That is the real trap in this plan shape: three a month is plenty for an occasional contract, and completely unworkable the one month you onboard two clients and fix a typo. The jump is $0 to $180/year with nothing in between.

Per-envelope cost, where the published numbers actually land

  • Cheapest published cost per envelope on a paid plan: Zoho Sign Standard. $10/user/month annually = $120/year for 25 envelopes a month (300 a year) = 40 cents per envelope.
  • Zoho Sign Standard beats Dropbox Sign Essentials until about 450 envelopes a year. $180 ÷ $0.40 = 450. Above that, flat-rate unlimited wins; below it, you are paying for headroom you are not using.
  • Cheapest unlimited-document plan: SignWell Light at $120/year, against Dropbox Sign Essentials at $180/year for the same unlimited promise — 50% more money for one sender either way. Zoho Sign Professional at $16/user/month annually ($192/year) is the other unlimited-envelope option, $12 a year above Dropbox Sign.
  • At $0, the ranking is: Zoho Sign 5 envelopes, then Dropbox Sign 3 requests and SignWell 3 documents, then Docusign with nothing. Dropbox Sign's edge over SignWell is the multi-document, multi-signer bundle counting as one request; SignWell's edge is that its free terms are printed on the pricing page where you can actually find them.

When each free plan is the right answer

  1. You sign your own documents and rarely request signatures. Dropbox Sign, on the stated-but-contradicted unlimited self-signing — verify it on a throwaway month, and remember self-signed documents come with no audit trail.
  2. You send four or five short agreements a month. Zoho Sign free, at 5 envelopes, is the only free plan of the four that covers you without a subscription.
  3. You want the free terms in writing before you commit. SignWell, whose free column is on the pricing page itself with every excluded feature visibly greyed out.
  4. You need a signature today and three a month forever after. Any of the three free plans. None of them is Docusign, which has no free tier to fall back to.
  5. You are sending enough that you will hit a cap. Price per envelope, not per month: Zoho Sign Standard at 40 cents is the cheapest published rate until roughly 450 a year, after which a flat unlimited plan is cheaper, and SignWell Light is the cheapest of those at $120/year.

Notice that none of those answers is "whichever is the biggest brand". The envelope maths crosses over in places that have nothing to do with reputation.

Verdict

The brief's number survived and almost nothing else did. Three signature requests a month is real, but it is a rolling 30-day allowance tied to your signup date, one Edit & Resend costs you a document, "unlimited signers" is not something the vendor publishes, and the audit trail is better than "basic" where it applies and entirely absent on self-signed documents. The product is not called HelloSign, its free plan is no longer documented on its pricing page, and two of the vendor's own help articles contradict each other on the single question that decides whether the free plan is generous or tight.

If you want an e-signature tool that costs nothing and will not run out on you, Zoho Sign's free plan is 5 envelopes a month against Dropbox Sign's 3 and Docusign's zero, and its cheapest paid tier is a third less money. If you want the terms of a free plan printed where a buyer can read them, SignWell wins on that alone. And if you are already in Dropbox and only need the occasional contract signed, the free plan is genuinely useful — just set a reminder for the day of the month it resets, because it will not be the 1st.

FAQ

Does HelloSign still exist, and does it still have a free plan?

The product is now called Dropbox Sign and lives at sign.dropbox.com; the HelloSign name does not appear on its pricing page. The free plan still exists: Dropbox Sign's help centre states it "lets you send 3 free signature requests per month", with unlimited self-signing noted in one article and contradicted in another.

How many documents can you send free on Dropbox Sign each month?

Three signature requests. The help centre is explicit that they "renew every 30 days from the day you sign up" and "do not refresh based on the calendar month", so the reset date is your signup anniversary, not the 1st. Using Edit & Resend on a document you already sent "will deduct another document from your available balance."

Does a Dropbox Sign free signature request cover multiple signers?

Yes, and this is the plan's best feature. The help centre defines a signature request as "the entire transaction of preparing and sending one or more documents to be electronically signed by one or more signers", and "as long as you send the document(s) to the signer(s) in a single bundle, it counts as only one signature request." No signer cap is published, which is not the same as the plan being unlimited. Bulk send via CSV is a Standard and Premium feature, not a free one.

Do you get an audit trail on the Dropbox Sign free plan?

For signature requests, yes: the help centre answers "Is the audit trail free too?" with "Yes. When your signature request is completed, you'll receive a PDF of the signed document(s) plus an audit trail." The exception is self-signed documents — those signed with the "Just Me" option "don't include an audit trail since you're the only signer."

How much does Dropbox Sign cost in 2026?

Essentials is $15 USD per month billed yearly ($180 USD a year) or $20 month-to-month, for one user and 5 templates. Standard is $25 USD per user per month billed yearly ("Starting at $600 USD billed yearly") or $30 monthly, for two or more users and 15 templates. Premium is quote-only. Both paid tiers advertise unlimited signature requests. There is no pay-per-document option: the help centre says so outright.

Which e-signature tool has the most generous free plan?

Of the four pages read on October 6 2026, Zoho Sign: US$0 for a single user with 5 envelopes a month, an audit trail, reminders and signature workflows. Dropbox Sign and SignWell both give 3 a month, and Docusign publishes no free plan at all in its eSignature grid. Per envelope on paid plans, Zoho Sign Standard at $10/user/month annually for 25 envelopes a month works out at 40 cents each.

Is Docusign's annual plan really cheaper?

Cheaper per month, smaller per year in what you can send. On the month-to-month subscription type Standard is $45/user/month with 10 envelopes per user per month (120 a year); on the annual commitment it is $30/user/month ($360) with 100 envelopes per user per year. Both numbers are printed in Docusign's own "Envelope sends" comparison row. That is 33% off the price and 17% off the allowance.
