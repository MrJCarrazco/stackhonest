Descript Transcription Pricing and Limits (2026): What the Free Tier Actually Meters

Last updated October 4, 2026 · ~11 min read · by Juan

Affiliate disclosure: some links below are affiliate links. If you sign up through them I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Every opinion here comes from actually running these tools — I will tell you when something is not worth your money.

Short answer: Descript's free tier gives you 60 media minutes per month — one hour — and that is a cap on media you upload or record, not on transcription. Transcription itself is uncapped and multilingual on every tier including Free; what runs out is your minute budget and your AI credits. The cheapest paid plan is Hobbyist at $16/mo billed annually, or $24/mo month-to-month, which raises you to 600 media minutes (10 hours). There is no $12 plan and no tier called Pro. Every number here was read off descript.com/pricing on October 4, 2026.

Three things the usual roundups get wrong about this, corrected

I started this page from a brief that made three specific claims about Descript's pricing. All three are wrong against the live page, and rather than quietly write around them I am going to name them, because the same three errors are repeated across most "Descript pricing" articles currently ranking.

  • Wrong: "the free tier provides 3 hours of transcription per month." The page says 60 media minutes — one hour, not three. And the unit is wrong, not just the quantity: Descript's own FAQ on that same page states media minutes "track uploaded media files and media recorded in Descript, regardless of whether they're transcribed." You can burn your whole hour on footage you never transcribe.
  • Wrong: "the Creator plan at $12/month gives 10 hours." Three errors in one sentence. Creator is $24/mo annual or $35/mo monthly. Creator carries 30 hours (1,800 media minutes), not 10. The 10-hour allowance is Hobbyist's, at $16/$24. No $12 price appears anywhere on the page, on either billing cycle.
  • Wrong: "the Pro plan for teams." There is no Descript plan called Pro. The tiers are Free, Hobbyist, Creator, Business and Enterprise. If an article is quoting you a Descript "Pro" price, it is working from a plan structure that does not exist in October 2026.

A fourth, softer correction: "unlimited Overdub." The word Overdub does not appear on the live pricing page at all, and nothing voice-related is described as unlimited. The feature set is listed as AI Speech — text-to-speech, Regenerate, custom voice clones — and it is metered out of monthly AI credits.

Two details worth more than the headline prices. First, Free's 100 AI credits are one-time, not monthly — the page prints "100 (one-time)" in the comparison table against "400 / month" for Hobbyist. Once they are gone on a free account they are gone. Second, credit and minute top-ups are listed on Creator and Business only. On Free and Hobbyist you hit a wall instead of an overage charge. That is arguably more honest billing than a surprise invoice, but it does mean a Hobbyist account can simply stop working mid-project in a heavy month.

Seat scaling is also tier-gated and a little odd: Hobbyist is a single seat, Creator "scales to a team of 3 (billed separately)", Business to 5. The minutes are per editor, so a three-person Creator team is paying $72/mo annually for 90 hours of combined media, not $24 for a pooled 30.

What is actually included on Free — and what the real wall is

This is where Descript is better than its reputation. Reading the comparison table row by row, the following are marked as present on every tier including Free:

  • Multi-language transcription, 25 languages — same count on Free as on Business.
  • Detect 8+ speakers, and multitrack transcription.
  • Unlimited projects, unlimited dynamic captions, unlimited shareable pages and links.
  • Unlimited audio export duration, and unlimited real-time recordings.

So "the free tier barely transcribes" is not the story. Transcription quality and language coverage are not the gate. The gates are:

  • 60 media minutes/month, counted on upload or recording, not on transcription.
  • 100 one-time AI credits, with the AI tools (Studio Sound, Remove Filler Words, Eye Contact, Green Screen, Edit for Clarity, generate image/video, the Underlord co-editor) marked "Limited" on Free and no top-ups available.
  • 720p export on Free (local and web link), 1080p on Hobbyist, 4K on Creator and Business.
  • Web-link export duration capped at 1 hour on Free and Hobbyist, 3 hours on Creator and Business — relevant if you publish long episodes as links.
  • 5 GB cloud storage on Free, 100 GB Hobbyist, 1 TB Creator, 2 TB Business; file upload size 1 GB → 10 → 20 → 50 GB.
  • Stock library search truncated: first 5 results on Free, first 12 on Hobbyist, unlimited on Creator and up. Which, incidentally, kills the "stock media is an add-on cost" framing — it is tier-gated access, not something you buy.

Descript's 60 minutes a month with no lifetime import cap is, in that narrow sense, the more usable free tier of the three.

When paying Descript's premium is the right call

It is the right call when the transcript is the editing surface rather than the deliverable. Concretely: you are cutting a video or a podcast, your edit decisions are sentence-level rather than waveform-level, and removing filler words, fixing retakes and generating captions in one pass is most of your post-production. That is a real workflow and no API gives it to you.

It is the wrong call if you are transcribing meetings, interviews or an archive for search and notes, where an API or Otter does the same job for a fraction. And it is the wrong call at Business tier for a solo operator: Business costs more than twice Creator ($50 vs $24 annual) for 10 extra hours of media, 700 extra credits and team features — the per-hour cost goes up, not down, as you climb from Creator to Business. That is the most counterintuitive thing on the whole price sheet and nobody flags it.

What I will not tell you about this

I have not run a transcription accuracy comparison between these tools, and I am not going to quote one. Every vendor on this page makes accuracy claims about its own models; AssemblyAI's page describes Universal-3.5 Pro as "the most accurate async speech-to-text model" and Universal-2 as trained on "over 12.5 million hours of audio." Those are marketing claims from the seller, not independent measurements, and treating them as benchmarks would be dishonest. There is no audited head-to-head word-error-rate test across Descript, Otter, Rev and AssemblyAI that I can point you to, so the only thing I will stand behind here is the arithmetic: published price divided by published allowance. Accuracy on your audio — your accents, your crosstalk, your room — is a thing you have to test on free tiers before you commit, and all four have one.

Likewise, I am not going to tell you media minutes roll over, because the page does not say they do.

Verdict

Descript Free is more capable than the "3 hours of transcription" myth suggests and more limited than it sounds: one hour of media a month, 100 credits total, 720p out. The honest upgrade path is Hobbyist $16/mo annual ($24 monthly) if you edit a few hours a month, Creator $24/$35 if you want 4K, 30 hours, top-ups and the full AI tool set — and that is also where Descript's per-hour cost bottoms out, so skipping straight past Hobbyist to Creator is usually the better buy. Skip Business unless you actually need the extra seats.

And if the transcript is the product rather than the interface, do not buy Descript at all. Descript earns its price as an editor. Transcription alone is a $0.15-$0.42 per hour problem, not a $0.80-$2.40 one.

FAQ

Does Descript's free plan include 3 hours of transcription per month?

No. As of October 4 2026 descript.com/pricing lists 60 media minutes -- one hour -- per month on the Free plan, and Descript's own FAQ on that page says media minutes track uploaded or recorded media regardless of whether they are transcribed. So it is a media-processing budget, not a transcription budget. The three-hour figure does not appear anywhere on the live pricing page. Separately, 25-language transcription, 8-plus speaker detection and multitrack transcription are all listed as included on every tier including Free.

Is there a Descript Creator plan at $12 a month?

No. The cheapest paid Descript tier on the live pricing page is Hobbyist at $16 per person per month billed annually, or $24 per person per month month-to-month. Creator is $24 annual / $35 monthly. There is no $12 price point on the page at any tier or billing cycle, and Creator carries 1,800 media minutes (30 hours) a month, not 10. The 10-hour allowance belongs to Hobbyist: 600 media minutes.

What are Descript's actual plan names in 2026?

Free, Hobbyist, Creator, Business and Enterprise. There is no tier called Pro. Verified October 4 2026 on descript.com/pricing: Free $0, Hobbyist $16/mo annual or $24 monthly, Creator $24/$35, Business $50/$65, Enterprise custom with a sales conversation. Annual billing is advertised on the page as saving up to 35 percent over monthly.

What is the cheapest way to transcribe audio if that is all I need?

A speech-to-text API, not a video editor. AssemblyAI's published pay-as-you-go rates on October 4 2026 are $0.15 per hour for Universal-2 and $0.21 per hour for Universal-3.5 Pro, with a free allowance the page states as up to 185 hours of pre-recorded transcription. For a point-and-click tool, Otter's Pro plan is $8.33 per user per month for 1,200 transcription minutes, which works out around $0.42 per hour. Descript's cheapest paid hour, on Creator, is roughly $0.80 -- and you are paying for the editor, not the transcript.

Is Overdub still unlimited on paid Descript plans?

The live pricing page does not use the name Overdub at all, and nothing about voice is described as unlimited. What it lists instead is AI Speech -- text-to-speech, Regenerate, custom voice clones and stock AI speakers -- metered out of your monthly AI credits: 100 one-time on Free, then 400, 800 and 1,500 per month on Hobbyist, Creator and Business. Top-ups for more credits are listed as available on Creator and Business only, which means Free and Hobbyist users hit a hard stop rather than an overage bill.

Does the media-minute cap reset or roll over?

The pricing page states the allowances as per-month figures per editor and says top-ups are available on Creator and Business. It does not publish a rollover policy for unused media minutes anywhere on the page, so I am not going to tell you one exists. If unused minutes carrying over matters to your budgeting, confirm it in writing with Descript support before you commit to annual billing -- annual is where you lose the ability to walk away cheaply.
