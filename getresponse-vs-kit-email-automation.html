GetResponse vs Kit for Email Automation (2026): Which Is Worth It

Last updated September 2026 · ~8 min read · by Juan

Short answer: GetResponse is a full marketing-automation suite -- conditional workflows, lead scoring, webinars, and unlimited landing pages once you are on a paid tier -- built for marketers running funnels, not just newsletters. Kit is built for creators who want to monetize a list directly: digital products, paid newsletters, and Tip Jars are native, and its free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, far past GetResponse free cap of 500 contacts. GetResponse Starter runs about 15.58 dollars per month annually for 1,000 contacts (no automation), Marketer about 48.38 dollars per month adds full automation and webinars. Kit Creator is 39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers (33 dollars per month annual). If you need webinars and complex funnel logic in one tool, GetResponse wins. If you are a creator selling to your own list and want the biggest free tier, Kit wins.

The 30-second answer

Get GetResponse if you need webinars, lead scoring, and multi-step marketing funnels in one platform. Get Kit if you are a creator who wants to sell digital products or a paid newsletter directly and wants the most generous free subscriber cap.

Pricing: verified against both vendor pages, September 2026

I checked getresponse.com/pricing and kit.com/pricing directly. GetResponse free plan covers 500 contacts, 1 landing page/website capped at 1,000 monthly visitors, and a 30-day trial of premium features before dropping to the reduced free tier -- no ongoing automation on the permanent free tier. Paid plans (billed annually, 1,000 contacts): Starter around 15.58 dollars per month (no automation, unlimited newsletters), Marketer around 48.38 dollars per month (adds automation, webinars for up to 10 attendees included, 5 seats), Creator around 56.58 dollars per month (5 websites, AI course creator selling enabled). Enterprise (Max) is custom pricing.

Kit has three tiers: Newsletter (free, up to 10,000 subscribers, 1 basic automation, no sequences), Creator (39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers, 33 dollars per month billed annually, unlimited automations and sequences, free migrations), and Creator Pro (79 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers, 66 dollars per month annually, adds subscriber scoring, deliverability reporting, SparkLoop referral network). Kit paid tiers scale with subscriber count: Creator runs 139 dollars per month at 10,000 subscribers.

Learning curve / ease of use

GetResponse packs more into one dashboard -- email, automation builder, landing pages, webinars, a course creator -- which means a steeper first week figuring out where things live, but you stop juggling separate tools sooner. Kit editor leans text-first and simple by design: fewer templates, no drag-and-drop page builder, built around writing plain emails fast rather than designing campaigns. If you want one tool that does everything for a funnel, expect to spend more time in GetResponse settings. If you just want to write and send, Kit gets out of your way faster.

Capability deep-dive: funnels vs creator monetization

GetResponse real strength is breadth: conditional automation workflows, lead scoring, unlimited landing pages and websites on paid tiers, and webinars bundled into the same subscription that would otherwise need a separate tool (Zoom, Demio, etc.). That makes it a genuine marketing-automation platform, not just an email sender. Kit real strength is depth in one direction: it is built specifically for creators who sell something to their list -- digital products, paid subscriptions, and Tip Jars are native, with a 3.5 percent plus 30 cent transaction fee, and Creator Pro adds the SparkLoop cross-promotion network so other creators can pay to be recommended to your list. GetResponse has no creator-monetization or cross-promotion equivalent; Kit has no webinar or lead-scoring equivalent. Pick based on which gap you actually have.

My own read: GetResponse and Kit are not really solving the same problem. GetResponse is a marketing-automation suite that happens to send email; Kit is an email and monetization tool for creators that happens to have basic automation. If you are running paid ads into landing pages with a multi-step nurture funnel, GetResponse breadth earns its price. If you are a creator whose main goal is to sell something directly to subscribers, Kit built-in monetization and bigger free tier make more sense.

Start with Kit free Newsletter plan if you are under 10,000 subscribers and want to monetize directly later. Start with GetResponse Marketer if you need webinars and real automation from day one.

FAQ

Is GetResponse or Kit better for email automation?

GetResponse has the deeper automation engine (conditional workflows, scoring, webinars, landing pages bundled in), which suits marketers running multi-step funnels. Kit is built for creators who want to monetize a list directly with digital products, paid newsletters, and Tip Jars, and its automation is simpler by design. If you need marketing-automation depth and webinars in one tool, GetResponse wins. If you are a creator selling to your own list and want the most generous free tier, Kit wins.

How much do GetResponse and Kit cost?

GetResponse free plan covers 500 contacts with 1 landing page capped at 1,000 monthly visitors, no ongoing automation. GetResponse Starter runs about 15.58 dollars per month annually, Marketer about 48.38 dollars per month, Creator about 56.58 dollars per month (all at 1,000 contacts, billed annually). Kit Newsletter plan is free for up to 10,000 subscribers with 1 basic automation. Kit Creator starts at 39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers (33 dollars per month annual), Creator Pro at 79 dollars per month (66 dollars per month annual).

Which has the better free plan?

Kit free Newsletter plan is more generous by subscriber count, up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, versus GetResponse free plan cap of 500 contacts. But GetResponse free plan includes a 30-day trial of premium features (webinars, automation, more landing pages) before dropping to the reduced free tier, so you get a fuller preview of the paid product up front.
