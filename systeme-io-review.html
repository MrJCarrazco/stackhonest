Short answer: yes, Systeme.io's free plan is genuinely free — a real tier with 2,000 contacts, 3 funnels, unlimited emails, and no credit card, not a 14-day trial with a countdown clock. What Systeme.io actually is

Systeme.io is an all-in-one funnel and marketing platform: landing pages, sales funnels, email broadcasts and sequences, course hosting, a blog, affiliate program management, and simple automation rules — one login, one bill (or no bill). The pitch is that it replaces a stack of ClickFunnels + Mailchimp + Teachable + a WordPress site.

I run automations for a living — my main stack is n8n plus AI tools — and my bar for recommending software is simple: does it do real work without becoming another subscription you resent? Systeme.io clears that bar, with caveats I'll get to.

The free plan, line by line

As of this writing (September 2026 — always verify on their pricing page, these things drift), the free plan includes:

  • 2,000 contacts — that's a real email list. Most people quit before they get 200 subscribers, let alone 2,000.
  • Unlimited emails — broadcasts and sequences to your whole list, no per-send metering.
  • 3 sales funnels — enough for a lead magnet funnel, a service/booking funnel, and one offer.
  • 1 course with unlimited students — if you ever package what you know.
  • 1 automation workflow + basic rules — "when someone opts in, tag them and start the sequence" territory.
  • Blog, order bumps, 1-click upsells, unlimited file storage — features competitors gate behind $97+/mo plans.

What you don't get free: evergreen webinars, custom domains beyond the first, advanced automation rules, and removing their branding in some spots. That's the honest catch — and notice none of it prevents you from launching.

What I actually use it for

My use case is the one I think fits it best: a service business front end. If you sell automation, consulting, design, or any done-for-you work, you need exactly four things — a landing page that states the offer, a lead magnet to capture emails, a follow-up sequence, and a way to book a call. Systeme.io's free plan does all four. My free n8n template pack funnel is exactly this pattern: opt-in page → email sequence → the emails point back to articles and offers.

Setting up the first funnel took me an afternoon, and most of that was writing copy, not fighting the tool. The funnel builder gives you proven page structures to start from, the email editor is basic but fine, and the automation rules are simple enough that you won't need a tutorial.

Where it breaks (production scars)

No honest review skips this section:

  • The page editor is rigid. Compared to Webflow or even Carrd, you're working within Systeme's blocks. Pixel-perfect brand design isn't happening. For a service funnel this genuinely doesn't matter — clarity converts, not custom CSS — but designers will grind their teeth.
  • Automation rules are shallow. If-this-then-that level. Complex branching logic belongs in a real automation tool — I pipe anything serious through n8n via webhooks and let Systeme handle only the email side. (See my n8n vs Zapier breakdown for how I think about where logic should live.)
  • Email deliverability is decent, not elite. I've had no disasters, but dedicated email platforms with strict list hygiene will edge it out. Warm up slowly and keep your list clean.
  • Support is email-based and can be slow on the free plan. Predictable, but worth knowing before launch day.

Who should skip Systeme.io

I promised honesty, so: skip it if you already pay for a stack that works (migration pain isn't worth it to save $27), if your brand lives or dies on custom design, if you need deep e-commerce (that's Shopify's job), or if your email program is sophisticated enough that deliverability tooling is your bottleneck.
