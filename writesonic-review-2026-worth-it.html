Writesonic Review (2026): Is It Worth It Now That It's a GEO Platform?

Last updated September 2026 · ~8 min read · by Juan

Affiliate disclosure: some links below are affiliate links. If you sign up through them I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Every opinion here comes from actually checking these tools -- I will tell you when something is not worth your money.

Short answer: Writesonic isn't the cheap AI article writer it used to be. Over 2025-2026 it quietly killed its old $13-99/mo content tiers and rebuilt itself as an "AI search visibility" (GEO) platform that tracks how your brand shows up inside ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews, starting at $79/mo. Article generation is still in there, but it's bundled as one feature of a bigger, pricier product now aimed at brands and agencies -- not solo bloggers who just want draft copy fast.

The 30-second verdict

Get Writesonic if you actually need to know whether ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews are recommending your brand or a competitor's, and you're willing to pay $79-399/mo for that visibility plus content tooling. Skip it if you just want an AI writer to draft blog posts cheaply -- that version of Writesonic effectively doesn't exist anymore, and you'll get more article output per dollar from a dedicated writer tool.

What actually changed

If you used Writesonic a year or two ago, the pricing page will not look familiar. The old structure -- a cheap Individual plan for solo creators, unlimited standard templates, a handful of advanced-template credits -- has been replaced by a four-tier "For Brands" track: Starter, Basic, Growth, and a custom Enterprise. The product's whole framing shifted from "AI writing assistant" to "AI Search Visibility Platform," and the pricing moved with it. This is a real repositioning, not just a price hike on the same product -- Writesonic is now selling brand-tracking and GEO (generative engine optimization) as the headline, with content generation as a supporting feature bundled inside.

The 3 things nobody mentions

  • The cheap plan is gone, not hidden. There's no $13-20/mo tier anymore for people who just want article drafts. If a listicle or comparison site still quotes old Writesonic pricing, it's stale -- check writesonic.com/pricing directly before you commit to a number you read somewhere else, including this article by the time you're reading it.
  • Article limits are tighter than the old "unlimited" framing. Starter gives 15 AI articles a month; if you publish daily, you'll blow through that by the second week and need Basic or Growth just for volume, on top of paying for visibility tracking you may not need yet.
  • Third-party review sites list different numbers. Some pricing aggregators show Writesonic starting at $249/mo for a "Basic" tier with different specs than the vendor's own $199 Basic plan. The vendor's own pricing page is the only source worth trusting -- third-party trackers lag or mix up plan names after a repositioning like this one.

Who should actually buy this now

  • Brands and agencies worried about AI-search visibility -- if leads are starting to come from ChatGPT or AI Overviews recommending competitors instead of you, this is built for exactly that problem, and $79/mo to find out is cheap compared to losing the deal.
  • Teams already producing content who want visibility tracking bundled in rather than running a separate GEO tool alongside a separate writer.
  • Skip it if: you're a solo blogger or affiliate site owner who just wants draft articles on a budget. You're paying for a visibility-tracking product you won't use, when a dedicated AI writer will get you more content per dollar.
