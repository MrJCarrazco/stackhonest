5 Cheaper ElevenLabs Alternatives for Voiceovers (2026 Pricing Tested)

Short answer: ElevenLabs is still the voice-quality leader in 2026, but if your bill is the problem, Cartesia Pro at $5/month (roughly 133 minutes of audio with a commercial license and instant voice cloning) and Fish Audio Plus at $15/month (up to 200 minutes) deliver most of the quality for a fraction of the per-hour cost. What ElevenLabs actually costs per finished hour

I checked elevenlabs.io/pricing again this week. The tiers: Free $0 (10,000 credits/mo, no commercial license), Starter $6/mo (30,000 credits, commercial license, instant voice cloning), Creator $22/mo (121,000 credits, professional voice cloning -- currently 50% off the first month), Pro $99/mo (600,000 credits). Credits burn at roughly 1,000 per minute of standard text-to-speech, so in practice:

  • Starter $6/mo → ~30 minutes → roughly $12 per finished hour
  • Creator $22/mo → ~2 hours → roughly $11 per finished hour
  • Pro $99/mo → ~10 hours → roughly $10 per finished hour

Note the pattern: the per-hour price barely drops as you spend more. There's no volume mercy until Enterprise. That ~$10-12/finished-hour figure is the number to beat, and it's why YouTubers doing daily uploads and audiobook producers go shopping. (One time-limited wrinkle: ElevenLabs is running a 3x-credits promo on Creator and up for its new v4 model until October 12 -- nice if you catch it, but I don't price long-term decisions on promos.) My full breakdown of the free tier's limits is in the ElevenLabs review. And if voice cloning is specifically what you're paying for, the voice cloning cost breakdown runs the Instant-vs-Professional math and the Starter/Creator break-even point.

One alternative you can cross off: Play.ht is gone. Meta acquired it in July 2025 and shut the platform down on December 31, 2025 -- accounts and API access terminated. The domain doesn't even resolve anymore (I checked). Our older ElevenLabs vs Play.ht comparison stays up for the record, but Play.ht is no longer an option at any price.

The 5 alternatives, ranked by value

1. Cartesia: the price-performance outlier

Cartesia's Sonic models are built for low-latency voice agents, but the same engine does narration well. The pricing is the story: Pro is $5/month for 100K credits -- the vendor's own calculator rates that at about 133 minutes of TTS -- and it includes a commercial-use license and instant voice cloning. That's over two hours of licensed audio for the price of a coffee; the equivalent spend on ElevenLabs buys you about 25 minutes. Startup at $49/mo (1.25M credits) adds professional voice cloning.

The honest cons: the voice library is smaller than ElevenLabs', and on highly emotional or acted reads the gap is audible -- Cartesia sounds clean and natural, ElevenLabs sounds performed. For straight narration, tutorials, and shorts, most listeners won't notice.

2. Fish Audio: best quality-per-dollar for creators

Fish Audio's free tier gives 8,000 credits a month (about 7 minutes -- enough to judge the quality, not enough to publish with). Plus at $15/month buys 250,000 credits, up to 200 minutes of generation, commercial use, voice cloning, and unlimited public voice slots. When I checked, an aggressive limited-time annual promo priced Plus at $5.50/mo ($66 billed annually) -- treat promos as promos, but even the $15 list price is a third of ElevenLabs Creator for roughly 1.7x the audio. Pro at $100/mo (1,620 minutes) undercuts ElevenLabs Pro on volume by a wide margin.

The honest cons: quality on its best S1 model is genuinely close to ElevenLabs on narration, but English prosody occasionally stumbles on long sentences, and the 500-character-per-generation cap on the free tier makes real testing tedious -- budget the first paid month as your trial.

3. Speechify Studio: cheapest per-hour hosted plan, annual-only catch

Speechify Studio's Starter is $100/year for 86,400 credits; voiceover burns 1 credit per second, so that's exactly 24 hours of generated audio a year with commercial rights -- about $4.20 per finished hour, among the cheapest hosted rates anywhere. Studio Creator at $300/year is 96 hours/year. The free plan (600 credits = 10 minutes) has no commercial rights and no cloning, so it's a demo only.

The honest cons: it's annual-billing-only -- there's no monthly off-ramp if you stop using it -- and the voices are tuned for clarity over character; fine for explainer and e-learning content, flat for storytelling. Don't confuse Studio with Speechify's $29/mo reading app; they're separate products.

4. Murf AI: the corporate-polish option

Murf's Studio Creator plan runs from $19/month on annual billing and includes 24 hours of voice generation per year with commercial rights; Business from $66/mo raises that to 96 hours and adds a business license. That works out to roughly $9.50 per finished hour -- barely cheaper than ElevenLabs -- so Murf isn't really the budget play here. What you're buying is the workflow: a timeline editor with per-word pronunciation control, emphasis, and stock media built in. Our ElevenLabs vs Murf head-to-head goes deeper on the quality gap.

The honest cons: the yearly generation cap (not monthly -- read it twice) ambushes anyone producing weekly content, and voices lean newsreader-professional rather than expressive.

5. Open-source TTS (Piper, Kokoro): free, unlimited, yours

If you've read this site's automation articles you know where this is going: the self-hosted option wins on economics the way self-hosted n8n wins against Zapier. Piper runs fast on a Raspberry Pi; Kokoro's quality punches far above its size and runs on a modest GPU or even CPU. Zero cost, zero metering, no license terms to re-read every quarter, and the output is yours. Pair one with an automation pipeline and you have a free voiceover factory -- the free n8n templates include the content-automation skeletons I'd bolt it onto.

The honest cons: setup is real work (Python or Docker, model files, an API wrapper if your editor needs one), voice cloning is hit-or-miss compared to the hosted tools, and nobody owes you support when it breaks at 11pm. The quality ceiling is also plainly below ElevenLabs -- excellent for utility audio, not for an audiobook you're selling.

Where ElevenLabs still wins

I run comparison sites, not a demolition derby -- so credit where due. ElevenLabs is still clearly ahead on: emotional range (acted dialogue, character work), professional voice cloning (the Creator-tier clone quality is what everyone else is chasing), language breadth with consistent quality, and ecosystem -- every video editor, dubbing tool, and agent framework integrates it first. If you're producing audiobooks or anything where the voice is the product, the ~$10/hour premium is usually worth it. If voice is just one component in a bigger automation stack, it usually isn't.

My recommendation for YouTubers and podcasters

  • Under ~30 min/month: stay on ElevenLabs Starter at $6/mo. At this volume nothing meaningfully undercuts it and you keep the best voices.
  • 1-4 hours/month, quality-sensitive: Fish Audio Plus at $15/mo. Closest to ElevenLabs quality at a third of the price.
  • Volume narration, shorts, faceless channels: Cartesia Pro at $5/mo -- the cheapest licensed audio per dollar of anything hosted.
  • E-learning / corporate, predictable annual output: Speechify Studio Starter ($100/yr) or Murf if you need the editing workflow more than the savings.
  • Technical + high volume: self-host Piper or Kokoro and spend the savings on better scripts.

FAQ

What is the cheapest ElevenLabs alternative in 2026?

Hosted with a commercial license: Cartesia Pro at $5/month (~133 minutes). Fish Audio Plus at $15/month covers up to 200 minutes. Self-hosted open-source models (Piper, Kokoro) are free with no metering.

Is ElevenLabs worth the price compared to alternatives?

For cloning quality, emotional range, and ecosystem, yes -- it's still the benchmark, and Starter at $6/mo is competitive at low volume. At scale it's ~$10-12 per finished hour while Cartesia and Fish Audio land at ~$2-5, which is hard to justify for plain narration.

What happened to Play.ht?

Meta acquired Play.ht (PlayAI) in July 2025 and shut the platform down December 31, 2025. Accounts, audio, and API access were terminated -- it's no longer an option.
