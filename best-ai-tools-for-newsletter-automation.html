Two corrections before anything else

This article started from a brief that said "Systeme.io's free plan limits the number of automation steps, which affects multi-step newsletter sequences." I checked that against systeme.io/pricing today and it is wrong in its specifics, in a way that matters if you are budgeting.

Correction 1: Systeme.io does not meter "automation steps." Its pricing page meters three separate things, and none of them is steps-per-workflow. On the Free plan you get 1 automation rule, 1 workflow, and 1 email campaign (campaign is Systeme.io's word for a sequence). Steps inside that one workflow are not listed as a metered limit anywhere on the page. So the wall is not "your sequence can only be 3 emails long" — it is "you only get one sequence, ever." That is a different and arguably worse constraint, and it is the one you will actually hit. Meanwhile email newsletters are unlimited and emails sent are unlimited on every tier including Free, up to the 2,000-contact cap.

Correction 2: Systeme.io has no AI writing features. This is an article about AI newsletter tools, so I have to be blunt about the tool I'd otherwise recommend first on price. I read systeme.io/pricing and systeme.io/features in full on October 3, 2026. Neither page lists an AI subject-line generator, an AI copy assistant, or any in-editor AI at all — the features page does not contain the term. If you want AI drafting your newsletter, Systeme.io is not the tool, and any roundup that puts it in an "AI newsletter tools" list without saying so has not read the vendor's own page. Systeme.io's case is that it bundles funnels, courses, and unlimited email for free. That case is strong. It is just not an AI case.

Building a signup-to-welcome sequence: where every free tier stops

This is the whole job. Someone subscribes, and within a minute they get email one; two days later email two; a week later email three. It is the only automation a newsletter genuinely needs on day one, and it is the thing almost every free tier withholds.

Stack the free plans against that single requirement and the picture is stark:

  • beehiiv Free: cannot do it. Live custom automations is a dash. You get a publication and unlimited broadcasts, and that's it.
  • Kit Free: cannot do it. Unlimited broadcasts to 10,000 people, but sequences and Visual Automations are on the Creator list. You can collect an enormous list for free and not greet any of them automatically.
  • MailerLite Free: can do it — 3 active automations is enough for a welcome sequence plus two more — but only to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails/month, which is about ten sends to a full list.
  • Systeme.io Free: can do it, once. One workflow, one rule, one email campaign. Build the welcome sequence and you have spent your entire automation budget, but you can mail 2,000 contacts unlimited times.
  • GetResponse Starter ($19/mo, $15.58 annual): one custom workflow. Same shape as Systeme.io Free, except you are paying for it — though Starter does include a prebuilt welcome email series separate from that workflow slot.

So the honest ranking for the welcome-sequence job specifically: Systeme.io Free if your list is under 2,000 and you only need one automation; MailerLite Free if your list is tiny but you want three automations and real AI-free room to grow; Kit Free if you are optimising for list size and will upgrade the moment you need a sequence. That last one is a legitimate strategy — collect 5,000 subscribers for $0, then pay $33/mo when automating them is worth $33/mo.

Using AI to generate subject lines and copy — what is actually on offer

Here is the part where I disappoint you on purpose.

What is real and written into the pricing pages: MailerLite's AI writing assistant (Comfort and Power, not Free). beehiiv's in-editor AI with a tiered credit allowance (Lite and up, not Free). GetResponse's AI email generator and AI subject-line generator, capped at "Up to 3 uses" on Starter and unlimited from Marketer. Kit's MCP connection to Claude/ChatGPT/Gemini, Creator and up. That is the complete, verifiable list across these five vendors. Everything else in this category's marketing is an LLM wrapper you could reproduce by pasting your draft into a chat window.

What I will not give you: a number for whether AI-written subject lines get opened more than ones you wrote yourself. There is no audited public benchmark for this. No vendor on this page publishes a controlled open-rate comparison of AI-generated versus human-written subject lines on its own platform, there is no independent test suite, and the figures that circulate in roundups ("AI subject lines lift opens 30%!") trace back to vendor blog posts or to nothing checkable at all. I have not run a controlled test either, and I am not going to invent one — a credible test needs thousands of subscribers split randomly across many sends, and anyone claiming it from a solopreneur list is making it up.

What I will say from the pricing pages alone is sharper anyway: on GetResponse, the tier where AI becomes unlimited is also the first tier where you could measure whether it helped. Starter gives you 3 AI uses and no A/B testing. Marketer at $59/mo gives you unlimited AI and the testing tools. The same pattern shows up at Kit, where A/B testing subject lines starts at Creator, and on MailerLite, where the AI writer and Smart sending both start at Comfort. The industry has quietly priced AI generation and AI measurement into the same upgrade. Draw your own conclusion about why.

My practical take after using these: AI is worth paying for when it writes the fifth variant of a subject line you already like, and worthless when it writes the first. Use it to break a blank page, never to decide what the email says.

Scheduling versus trigger-based sending

Two completely different mechanisms that roundups mush together, and the price difference between them is the real story of this category.

Scheduled sends (broadcasts) are nearly free everywhere. Kit: unlimited broadcasts on Free, no sending limit on any plan. beehiiv: unlimited email sends on every tier including Free. Systeme.io: unlimited emails sent and unlimited newsletters on every tier. MailerLite is the exception that proves the rule — it meters sends by plan (2,500/mo Free, 5,000/mo Comfort, unlimited on Power), which is unusual in 2026 and worth knowing before you pick it.

Trigger-based sends (automations) are where every vendor puts the wall, and they put it in a different place:

  • Kit gates on existence: none on Free, unlimited on Creator. No middle.
  • MailerLite gates on count: 3 → 50 → unlimited.
  • beehiiv gates on count and trigger sophistication: none → 10 → unlimited, with click-triggered automations and A/B-split branching held back for Pro.
  • Systeme.io gates on count, severely at the bottom: 1 → 10 → 100 → unlimited rules.
  • GetResponse gates on count: 1 custom workflow on Starter → unlimited on Marketer.

If your newsletter is genuinely just "I write a thing, it goes out Tuesday," you never need to pay any of these people for automation, and you should not. The upgrade is justified the day you have something to sell on a timer — a course launch, an evergreen funnel, a re-engagement series. If you are building that funnel side of things rather than just the sending side, the overlap with funnel builders is covered in Systeme.io vs Kit for creators, which goes deeper on the same two tools from the business-model angle.

Deliverability in free versus paid tiers

Deliverability is the section where this category lies most, so this one is short and only contains things printed on vendor pages.

No vendor on this page publishes an audited inbox-placement rate. "Industry-leading deliverability" appears in beehiiv's own Free-plan feature list as a marketing phrase with no number attached to it, and that is representative of all five. There is no third-party audit of these platforms' inbox placement that I can point you at, so treat every deliverability claim in this category — including the ones in the roundups ranking these tools — as unverifiable.

What is on the pages, and does affect whether you land in the inbox:

  • Branding on your emails. MailerLite Free keeps the MailerLite logo on your sends; removing it starts at Comfort. beehiiv Free keeps beehiiv branding; removal is a Pro feature ($95/mo yearly), not Lite. Kit removes its branding at Creator. Branded footers on a free tier are a visible signal that you are on a shared free-tier sending pool.
  • Authentication. GetResponse's comparison table shows Custom DKIM on every paid tier including Starter, which is the single most useful deliverability feature in this list and the one I would actually pay for.
  • Send-time optimisation. MailerLite's Smart sending is Comfort and up. GetResponse's Perfect timing and Time travel are unavailable on Starter and start at Marketer.
  • Dedicated IPs. Enterprise-only at beehiiv and GetResponse. Irrelevant to a solopreneur, and if a salesperson tells you otherwise at your list size, they are upselling you.
  • Deliverability reporting. At Kit this is a Pro ($66/mo yearly) feature. You cannot see your own deliverability data on Free or Creator.

The honest summary: you cannot buy your way to the inbox at these prices, and the free tiers' real deliverability penalty is a branded footer plus no visibility into what happened. Authenticate your domain, mail people who asked for it, and stop reading deliverability rankings.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to automate a newsletter welcome sequence in 2026?

Systeme.io's free plan, if your list is under 2,000 contacts: it includes 1 automation rule, 1 workflow and 1 email campaign at $0, with unlimited sends. That is exactly enough for one welcome sequence and nothing more. If you want more than one automation, MailerLite's free plan allows 3 — but caps you at 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month. Verified against both vendors' pricing pages on October 3, 2026.

Does Kit's free plan include automations?

No. As of October 3, 2026 kit.com/pricing lists unlimited landing pages, forms and email broadcasts on Free up to 10,000 subscribers, but unlimited Visual Automations and unlimited email sequences are on the Creator plan — $33/mo billed yearly ($390/year) or $39/mo month-to-month at 1,000 subscribers. Kit MCP, A/B testing subject lines and removing Kit branding are also Creator features.

Which newsletter tool has the best AI writing feature?

By what is actually written on the pricing pages: MailerLite includes an AI writing assistant from Comfort ($10.80/mo yearly, $12/mo monthly) and is the cheapest route to it; GetResponse has the most complete AI set — a full email generator plus a subject-line generator — but caps both at 3 total uses on Starter and only unlocks them properly at Marketer ($48.38/mo annual, $59/mo monthly); beehiiv's in-editor AI starts at Lite ($49/mo yearly, $59/mo monthly). Systeme.io has no AI writing feature at any price. Which is "best" in output quality I cannot tell you, because there is no audited comparison and I have not run a controlled one.

Do AI-generated subject lines actually get higher open rates?

There is no audited public number that answers this. None of the vendors on this page publishes a controlled test of AI-generated versus human-written subject lines on its own platform, and no independent benchmark exists. The percentages that circulate in roundups trace back to vendor marketing. Treat the AI generators as a speed tool for producing variants, not as a proven performance lift, and A/B test on your own list if the decision matters to you.

Does Systeme.io's free plan limit how many steps a newsletter sequence can have?

No — that is a common misstatement. Systeme.io's pricing page meters automation rules (1 on Free), workflows (1 on Free) and email campaigns (1 on Free), not the number of steps inside a workflow. It also lists 15 sales-funnel steps on Free, which is a separate funnel-builder limit and not an email limit. Emails sent and email newsletters are both unlimited on every tier, including Free, up to the plan's 2,000-contact ceiling. Verified October 3, 2026.

Is beehiiv's free plan usable for a real newsletter?

For publishing, yes — 2,500 subscribers and unlimited sends, plus a website and podcast hosting. For automating, no: "Live custom automations" is a dash on the Free column, so there is no welcome sequence at all, and beehiiv's own branding stays on your emails until you reach Pro at $95/mo billed yearly, not Lite. If you need a triggered sequence, Lite is $49/mo yearly or $59/mo monthly.
