Wave Free Invoicing (2026): Unlimited Invoices, but Receipts Are a Paid Add-On

Last updated October 5, 2026 · ~11 min read · by Juan

Disclosure: I am not in an affiliate program with Wave or any accounting tool, so every link on this page is a plain editorial link to the vendor's own pricing page. Other pages on this site do carry affiliate links and say so clearly. Nothing here is sponsored.

Short answer: Wave's free tier — which is called Starter, not "Free" — does give you genuinely unlimited invoices, estimates, bills and bookkeeping records at $0/month. It does not give you receipt capture: digitally capturing receipts is a paid add-on at $8/month or $72/year (US) on Starter, and is only bundled free with the Pro plan at $19/month or $190/year. The thing that actually costs most solo operators money is not the subscription at all: it is Wave's card fee of 2.9% + $0.60 per transaction, where the 60-cent fixed component is double the 30 cents most processors charge. Every figure below was read off Wave's own pages on October 5, 2026.

The premise I had to correct: "unlimited invoices and receipts" is half right

This page started from a one-line brief: "Wave's free invoicing plan lets you send unlimited invoices and receipts without a monthly fee." Part of that holds up exactly. Part of it does not, and it is the part that quietly costs $96/year.

  • Correct: unlimited invoices, at $0. The Starter plan card on waveapps.com/pricing reads "Create unlimited estimates, invoices, bills, and bookkeeping records" and the price is $0 with no trial clock attached. No invoice count, no client count, no annual cap. That is rarer than it sounds — see the Zoho Invoice section below, where "free" means 500 invoices a year.
  • Wrong: receipts. In Wave's own full feature-comparison table, "Digitally capture unlimited receipts" sits under the heading Optional add-ons and is priced at $8/month OR $72/year for US accounts ($11/month or $96/year CAD). The Pro column shows it as included. So on the free plan, receipt capture is a paid product, not a free feature. You can still type expenses in by hand for free; what you cannot do for free is photograph a receipt and have Wave's OCR turn it into a bookkeeping record.
  • Also worth naming: the plan is not called "Free." Wave's two self-serve tiers are Starter and Pro. Articles that compare "Wave Free vs Wave Pro" are using a name that is not on the pricing page.

That distinction matters because receipt capture is exactly the feature a sole trader shops for. If you adopted Wave because you read it was free and you wanted your shoebox of receipts digitised, you are $96/year away from what you thought you signed up for — or $190/year if you take Pro, which bundles it.

Two things on that page I would not act on without checking at checkout.

First, there is a promo banner on the Pro card reading "$9.50 USD/month, save $28.50 off your first 3 months." The footnote attached to it states the offer is "available from December 2nd, 2025 until January 6th, 2026." As of October 5 2026 that window has closed, yet the promo is still rendering on the live pricing page. I do not know whether the offer is genuinely still honoured or the banner is simply stale — so treat the $19 monthly price as the one you will pay, and treat $9.50 as a thing to confirm at checkout rather than budget around.

Second, the Pro price is per business, not per owner. Wave's own FAQ spells this out: one account can hold several business profiles, and putting three of them on Pro means paying $19 three times.

The real cost of Wave is the payment fee, not the plan

Accepting money through a Wave invoice is a pay-per-use feature on both plans, and this is where the money actually goes. Rates from waveapps.com/payments, October 5 2026:

Read the Pro column carefully, because the plan card advertises "2.9% + $0**" in large type and puts the limit in a footnote. The footnote says the $0 fixed fee applies to "the first ten cumulative transactions in a monthly subscription period," after which you are back on Starter's rates. If you send 40 invoices a month, 30 of them cost the same on Pro as they do on the free plan.

The 60-cent fixed fee is the part worth sitting with. Most card processors charge 30 cents; Wave charges double that on Starter. On a small invoice that is not a rounding error:

What the free plan is actually missing

Going down Wave's comparison table row by row, here is what is absent or conditional in the Starter column:

  • Auto-import of bank transactions (via Plaid), and auto-merge/categorise of those transactions. Both are Pro-only. On Starter you enter transactions manually.
  • Removing Wave branding from invoice footers. Pro only. Adding your own logo and brand colours is included on Starter, which is a more generous split than most free tiers.
  • Attachments on invoices and estimates, and reusable saved message templates. Pro only.
  • Adding users to your account as admin/editor/viewer. Pro only — though Wave notes a payroll manager or Block Advisors tax pro can be added free.
  • Third-party integrations. Pro only.
  • Sending invoices automatically through Wave, accepting deposits on estimates, and automated late-payment reminders (3, 7 and 14 days after due). All three read "with online payments" on Starter — meaning you get them once you have been approved for payments, not as plain plan features.
  • Live chat and email support reads "with any optional add-on" on Starter. On the free plan with nothing attached, you are on self-serve help docs.
  • Managing transactions in the mobile app requires the receipts add-on on Starter.

What Starter does include and people underrate: unlimited invoices, estimates and bills; unlimited bookkeeping records; branded invoice templates; invoicing from the mobile app; and the dashboard with profit-and-loss reporting. For a one-person service business that bills a handful of clients and does its own data entry, that is a complete product at $0.

Payroll add-on costs

Wave Payroll is a separate monthly charge on top of whichever plan you are on. From waveapps.com/payroll, October 5 2026:

  • US: $40/month base fee + $6 per active employee + $6 per independent contractor paid.
  • Canada: $25 CAD/month base + $6 per active employee + $6 per contractor paid. Available in all provinces and territories except Quebec.

So a US sole proprietor paying themselves plus one contractor is $40 + $6 + $6 = $52/month, which is $624/year — more than three times the Pro subscription. Payroll is the expensive part of Wave by a wide margin, and it is worth pricing against dedicated payroll providers rather than defaulting to it because it is in the same dashboard.

One pricing-history note Wave publishes itself and most reviews miss: before April 2 2025 its tax service was not available in every US state, and the base rate was higher in the states where it was. Wave now offers the tax service in all 50 states at one price, which it states plainly means the base price "will increase for states that now have access." If you have been on Wave Payroll since 2024 in a non-tax-service state, your base fee went up as a result of that change, not as a general price rise.

The tax service itself covers automatic payment and filing of state and federal payroll taxes, unlimited free direct deposits, and W-2/1099 generation. Wave advertises a "100% payroll accuracy guarantee"; I have not read the terms of that guarantee, so I am not characterising what it actually covers.

Where Wave's free tier beats and loses to the obvious alternatives

Zoho Invoice — free, but with caps Wave does not have

Zoho Invoice is genuinely $0 with no paid upgrade path inside the product, and it is the stronger comparison to make because people assume "free invoicing" means the same thing everywhere. It does not. Zoho's own FAQ states the free account gives you up to two users, a maximum of three projects, and up to 500 invoices per year, with "Powered by Zoho Invoice" branding on documents, and allows up to two organisations per account. Wave puts no number on invoices at all.

So: Zoho Invoice wins if you want a second user without paying (Wave charges Pro for that) and if 500 invoices a year is comfortably above your volume. Wave wins if you invoice heavily, or if you want bookkeeping and invoicing in one ledger rather than invoicing alone. Zoho also notes a data-retention policy that deletes data after 180 days of inactivity plus a 20-day grace period — relevant if you invoice seasonally.

Square Invoices — free plan, cheaper fixed fee, worse percentage

Square's free tier is $0/month per location and lists invoices and recurring invoices, estimates, contracts and projects. Its card rates are 2.6% + 15¢ for tap/dip/swipe and 3.3% + 30¢ online. Square Plus is $49/month per location (2.5% + 15¢ in person, 2.9% + 30¢ online, and an ACH fee capped at $10); Square Premium is $149/month (2.4% + 15¢, 2.9% + 30¢ online).

Against Wave Starter's online card rate of 2.9% + $0.60, Square's 3.3% + 30¢ has a higher percentage and a lower fixed fee, so there is a crossover. Setting 0.029x + 0.60 equal to 0.033x + 0.30 gives x = $75: below a $75 invoice, Square's online rate costs less; above it, Wave's costs less. On a $40 invoice Wave takes $1.76 and Square takes $1.62; on a $1,000 invoice Wave takes $29.60 and Square takes $33.30. If your average invoice is tiny, that gap is your margin.

I did not find a stated invoice-count limit on Square's free plan, so I am not claiming it has one or that it is unlimited — the page lists the features without quantifying them.

Who should actually use Wave Starter, and who should not

  • Use it: you are a solo service business, you invoice a few clients a month in the hundreds-of-dollars range, you are happy entering bank transactions by hand, and you want invoicing and bookkeeping in the same ledger for tax time. At $0 with unlimited invoices, nothing in this category undercuts it.
  • Pay for Pro: you want receipts captured by OCR and bank transactions imported automatically. Receipts alone is $72/year as an add-on; Pro is $190/year and bundles receipts, bank import, extra users, branding removal and attachments. If you only want receipts, the add-on is the cheaper answer and most reviews skip past it.
  • Look elsewhere: your invoices are large and paid by bank transfer. Wave's uncapped 1% ACH is the single worst line in its pricing at four figures and up.
  • Look elsewhere: your invoices are very small and paid by card. The 60-cent fixed fee is double the industry norm and lands hardest exactly where you can least absorb it.

What I could not verify, and will not guess

  • Whether the $9.50/month Pro promo is still live. Its own terms say it ran 2 December 2025 to 6 January 2026, yet the banner still renders in October 2026. I cannot tell from the page whether it is honoured or stale, so I have priced Pro at $19/$190.
  • What Wave's "100% payroll accuracy guarantee" actually pays out. The claim appears on the payroll page without terms attached on that page, so I am describing it as a claim and nothing more.
  • Payment-processing approval. Wave states approval is subject to identity verification and credit review, and that it may hold funds. I cannot tell you your odds of approval, and neither can anyone writing one of these pages.
  • Square's free-plan invoice volume. No number is published on the page I read, so none appears above.
  • Whether invoicing software makes you get paid faster. Wave states customers who enable online payments mark invoices paid three times faster than cash or cheque. That is Wave's own figure about its own customers, not an independent study, and I am reporting it as a vendor claim rather than adopting it. No audited benchmark comparing invoicing tools on payment speed exists that I could find, so there is no number of mine anywhere on this page.
  • Non-US/CA pricing. Wave's page geo-switches between USD and CAD only. Everything above is the US column unless marked CAD.

Verdict

Wave Starter is one of the few genuinely unlimited free invoicing plans, and the brief that sent me here was right about that half. It was wrong about receipts, which cost $8/month or $72/year on the free plan and are only bundled at Pro. The bigger story is that Wave's subscription is cheap and its payment fees are not: 2.9% + $0.60 on cards with a fixed fee twice the norm, and an uncapped 1% on bank payments that turns a $5,000 invoice into a $50 charge. Pick Wave for the ledger, not for the processing — and if you can get clients onto bank payments for small invoices and cards for large ones, you have inverted the fee curve in your favour.

FAQ

Is Wave invoicing really free?

Yes, for invoicing itself. Wave's Starter plan is $0 per month and its plan card states you can create unlimited estimates, invoices, bills and bookkeeping records, with no invoice count, client count or trial clock. What is not free on Starter is digital receipt capture, which is an add-on at $8/month or $72/year in the US, and payment processing, which is 2.9% + $0.60 per card transaction or 1% per bank payment with a $1.00 minimum.

Does Wave's free plan include receipt scanning?

No. On waveapps.com/pricing as of October 5 2026, "Digitally capture unlimited receipts" appears under the heading "Optional add-ons" priced at $8/month or $72/year for US accounts ($11/month or $96/year CAD), and is shown as included only on the Pro plan. You can still record expenses manually on Starter for free; the OCR capture is what costs money.

How much does Wave Pro cost in 2026?

$19/month billed monthly, or $190/year billed annually, which works out to $15.83/month and matches the "Save $38" badge on the page. Canadian pricing is $25 CAD/month or $250 CAD/year. The fee is per business, not per owner, so running three businesses on Pro means paying three times. A promotional $9.50/month price for the first three months is still displayed, but its own footnote says the offer ran from December 2 2025 to January 6 2026 — confirm it at checkout rather than assuming it.

What are Wave's payment processing fees?

On Starter: 2.9% + $0.60 per Visa, Mastercard or Discover transaction, 3.4% + $0.60 for American Express, and 1% per bank payment with a $1.00 minimum fee. On Pro the fixed card fee drops to $0, but Wave's footnote limits that to the first ten cumulative transactions in each monthly subscription period, after which Starter rates resume. The 60-cent fixed fee is double the 30 cents most processors charge, which makes small invoices disproportionately expensive — a $50 card payment costs 4.10% all-in.

How much does Wave Payroll cost?

In the US, $40/month base plus $6 per active employee and $6 per independent contractor paid. In Canada, $25 CAD/month base plus the same $6 per person, available in every province and territory except Quebec. A US owner paying themselves and one contractor is $52/month, or $624/year. Wave also notes that since it extended its tax service to all 50 US states on April 2 2025, the base price increased for states that previously did not have it.

Is Zoho Invoice a better free option than Wave?

It depends on volume and users. Zoho Invoice is free with no paid tier, and its FAQ states the free account supports up to two users, a maximum of three projects and up to 500 invoices per year, with "Powered by Zoho Invoice" branding. Wave puts no cap on invoice count but charges for a second user via Pro. Zoho wins on multi-user and loses on volume; Wave wins if you invoice heavily or want bookkeeping in the same ledger.
