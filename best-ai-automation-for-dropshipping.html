First, a correction to the premise everyone repeats

Almost every "automate your dropshipping store" post you will read says some version of "n8n's free tier caps your workflow executions, so high-volume stores need a paid plan." I went to check that sentence against the source before building a cost table on top of it, and it is wrong in both directions.

As of October 3, 2026, n8n.io/pricing does not list a free cloud tier at all. The cloud plans are Starter, Pro, Business, and Enterprise, and the entry point is a free trial, not a free plan. Meanwhile the thing that actually is free — n8n Community Edition, which you self-host — has no execution cap whatsoever. So there is no "free tier with an execution cap." There is a paid cloud tier with an execution cap, and a free self-hosted edition with none.

That matters for a dropshipping store specifically, because order volume is the one number that grows while your margins do not. Paying per execution is the wrong shape of bill for that. Here is what each platform actually charges, straight off the pricing pages today.

How order sync actually works between store and supplier

Strip the marketing off and there are only three moving parts, and two of them are not AI at all.

  1. Order in. Your store fires a webhook the moment an order is paid. Shopify, WooCommerce, and eBay all do this natively. This is a push — nothing polls, nothing is delayed, and it costs you one execution/credit/task.
  2. Order out to the supplier. Either the app already speaks your supplier's API (DSers' auto-place orders, AutoDS' order automation) or you are POSTing to the supplier's endpoint yourself, or — the ugly common case — you are generating a CSV and emailing it to a human at the supplier.
  3. Tracking back in. The supplier eventually produces a tracking number; something has to pull it and write it to the order so the customer-facing notification fires. DSers calls this auto sync tracking number and caps the sync window by plan (14 days on Advanced, 21 days on Pro and Enterprise). That cap is the detail people miss — a slow-boat supplier can hand you a tracking number after your plan has stopped looking for it.

None of that is AI. It is webhooks, scheduled jobs, and field mapping, and it has worked the same way for a decade. If you only need those three steps and your supplier is on AliExpress or 1688, a dropshipping app does all three out of the box and you should not be building it in a workflow tool. The honest reason to reach for n8n or Make is step 2 going wrong: a supplier with no connector, a supplier who only accepts email, or a second supplier you want to fail over to when the first is out of stock. That is glue work, and glue work is what those platforms are for.

Where AI genuinely shows up — and where vendors are overselling it

I want to be specific here because "AI dropshipping automation" is one of the most oversold phrases of 2026.

Real, and metered on the pricing page: product-copy generation and image work. DSers lists "AI Optimize Product Info" with a hard monthly allowance — 50 optimizations a month on Pro, 200 on Enterprise, and it is not listed on the free Basic tier at all. AutoDS includes title and description optimization starting at its Starter tier ($39.90/mo), and both vendors ship AI image tools. This is the layer where AI is doing something a human would otherwise do badly at scale: rewriting 400 supplier titles that read like 2026 New Fashion Women Summer Casual Loose into something a buyer would click.

Also real: using an LLM node inside a workflow to classify inbound supplier emails ("is this a shipping delay, a stockout, or a price change?") and route them. That is a genuinely good use of a model, it is cheap, and it fails safely — a misclassified email gets looked at by you, it does not move money.

What I will not give you a number for: whether AI-driven order routing or AI stock prediction actually reduces errors or stockouts. I looked for a published, audited benchmark on this and there isn't one. No vendor publishes error rates for its own fulfillment automation, there is no independent test suite, and the numbers that circulate in affiliate roundups ("reduces errors by 90%!") trace back to vendor marketing copy or to nothing at all. So I am not going to invent a figure to fill a section. Judge these tools on what their pricing pages commit to in writing — store counts, sync windows, allowances — because those are the only claims you can actually hold them to.

The practical rule I use: let AI touch text and classification, never the step that decides what gets purchased or shipped. Keep money movement on deterministic rules you can read.

Reducing manual order entry: the build, in order

If you are going the workflow-tool route for a supplier with no connector, this is the shape that holds up. It is four nodes, not forty.

  1. Webhook trigger on order paid. Not a schedule. A scheduled poll on Make's Free plan cannot run more often than every 15 minutes, which means a customer can be waiting a quarter of an hour before anything happens.
  2. Normalise the payload — one step that maps store fields to supplier fields and nothing else. Keep it separate from the send step so that when the supplier changes a field name you have one obvious place to fix.
  3. Send to supplier, with retry. This is the step that will fail, and it will fail at 3am. Per-node retry plus a global error workflow is the difference between a delayed order and a silently lost one — I wrote the exact settings up in n8n error handling and retry workflows, and the same logic applies on Make.
  4. Write tracking back when it arrives, and alert yourself when it hasn't arrived in N days. The missing-tracking alert is worth more than the automation itself; it is how you catch a supplier who has quietly stopped shipping.

One structural warning on step counts, because it is the single biggest cost difference between platforms: that four-step build costs one n8n execution, but four Make credits and four Zapier tasks, per order. n8n charges per workflow run "regardless of complexity" — that is their own wording on the pricing page. Make charges per module action. Zapier charges per step, and its 2026 pricing page now explicitly pools AI steps, code, MCP and SDK usage into the same task budget. Adding error handling and an alert branch makes your workflow better and, on two of the three platforms, more expensive. Plan accordingly.

The platform cost nobody puts in the comparison

Your automation bill is a rounding error next to your store bill, and roundups never mention it. Verified on shopify.com/pricing today: Basic is $29/mo on yearly billing but $39/mo paid monthly, Grow is $79/$105, Advanced is $299/$399, and Plus starts at $2,300/mo. Card rates run from 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic down to 2.25% + 30¢ on Plus.

At 500 orders a month with a $30 average order value — $15,000 in sales — the payment-processing spread between Basic and Advanced is about $96/mo, which is several times larger than any automation subscription on this page. If you are optimising the $19.90 line item while paying monthly instead of yearly on Shopify Basic, you are counting the wrong number.

What I would actually do

  • Starting out, AliExpress supplier, under 3,000 products: DSers Basic, free. Run it manually for a month so you learn where your supplier actually breaks before you automate around it.
  • Doing real volume on AliExpress/1688: DSers Advanced at $19.90/mo for auto-place orders and auto stock sync. This is the default right answer and it is cheap.
  • Multiple marketplaces or you want the AI copy rewriting bundled: AutoDS Starter at $39.90/mo. You are paying double for coverage breadth, which is a fair trade if you actually sell on eBay and Amazon too.
  • A supplier with no connector, or custom internal alerting: add Make Core at $12/mo if you want visual and hosted, or self-host n8n on a $6.49/mo VPS if you want uncapped and are comfortable owning a server. My own glue runs on self-hosted n8n for exactly the reason in the cost table: the bill does not move when the volume does.
  • Skip entirely: any tool pitching "AI-powered" order routing without a published allowance or limit on its pricing page. If the AI is real, it is metered, because inference costs the vendor money. Unmetered "AI" in a feature list is usually a rules engine with a new label.

FAQ

Does n8n have a free plan for dropshipping automation?

Not on n8n Cloud. As of October 3, 2026, n8n.io/pricing lists no free cloud tier — only a free trial, then Starter at $20/mo billed annually ($24/mo month-to-month) for 2,500 workflow executions. The genuinely free option is n8n Community Edition, which you self-host, and it has no execution cap at all. Your cost there is the server: a Hostinger KVM 1 VPS is $6.49/mo on the promotional 2-year term and renews at $11.99/mo.

What does dropshipping order automation cost for 500 orders a month?

It depends entirely on what the tool meters. Dropshipping apps bill per store and catalog size, not per order — DSers Advanced is $19.90/mo and AutoDS Starter is $39.90/mo for 201–500 products, so 500 orders changes nothing. General platforms bill per run: n8n counts one execution per whole workflow (500 of Starter's 2,500, at $20–24/mo), Make counts one credit per module action (a six-action workflow is ~3,000 of Core's 10,000 credits, $12/mo annual), and Zapier counts a task per step, so the same job needs a higher task tier than its entry price covers.

Can AI actually automate dropshipping inventory and order sync?

The reliable parts are not AI — they are scheduled API syncs and webhooks, and they have worked for years. The genuinely AI parts in 2026 are product-copy rewriting and image cleanup: DSers meters AI Optimize Product Info at 50/month on Pro and 200 on Enterprise, and AutoDS includes title and description optimization from Starter up. Treat AI as the copywriting and classification layer, not the money-movement layer. No vendor publishes an audited accuracy benchmark for AI-driven order routing, so there is no credible number to quote — and I am not going to make one up.

Should I use a dropshipping app or a general automation platform?

Use a dropshipping app if your suppliers are AliExpress, 1688, or Alibaba — DSers and AutoDS already know those APIs, and the free or $19.90 tier beats building it yourself. Use n8n or Make when your supplier has no connector and only sends email or CSV, because that is exactly the glue those platforms exist for. Most stores past a few hundred orders end up running both.

Is Make's free plan enough to run fulfillment?

No. Two separate reasons: 1,000 credits a month runs dry around order 165 on a six-action workflow, and the Free plan enforces a 15-minute minimum interval between runs, which is unacceptable for anything a customer is waiting on. Treat Make Free as a place to build and test, then move to Core at $12/mo annual before you point real orders at it.
