Short answer: Kit free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, more generous than any other free tier in this category, but caps you at 1 basic automation and no sequences. Kit Creator is 39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers (33 dollars per month annual), and Creator Pro is 79 dollars per month (66 dollars per month annual), adding subscriber scoring, deliverability reporting, and the SparkLoop referral network. MailerLite free plan is far tighter, 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails per month, but its paid tiers are cheaper: Comfort starts at 19 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers, Power at 39 dollars per month. Kit wins if you plan to monetize your list directly since that is built in. MailerLite wins on raw price once you are past the free tier and do not need Kit creator-monetization tools.

The 30-second answer

Get Kit if you sell digital products, run a paid newsletter, or want the largest free-tier subscriber cap (10,000) before paying anything. Get MailerLite if you want the cheapest paid automation and landing-page tool and do not need built-in monetization.

Learning curve / ease of use

Kit editor leans text-first, only 3 email templates and no visual drag-and-drop builder, which some creators find limiting but others prefer because it forces plain, readable emails that avoid the promotional look of heavily designed campaigns. MailerLite has a proper drag-and-drop visual builder with more template variety, which makes it faster to put together a polished-looking campaign without design skill. If visual design matters more to you than monetization tooling, MailerLite editor is the more comfortable starting point.

Capability deep-dive: monetization vs price

Kit real differentiator is that it is built for creators who sell something: digital products, paid newsletter subscriptions, and Tip Jars are native features (with a 3.5 percent plus 30c transaction fee), and Creator Pro unlocks the SparkLoop-powered recommendation network where other creators can pay you to recommend their newsletter to your subscribers, or you can cross-promote for free. MailerLite has no equivalent monetization layer, it is a straightforward email, automation, and landing-page tool, and if you want to sell digital products you will need a separate platform (Systeme.io, Gumroad, etc.) bolted on. Where MailerLite pulls ahead: price at every subscriber tier, a real visual editor, and a more generous set of included integrations (100+, including ChatGPT and Claude) even on the free plan.

Where each one breaks in production

  • Kit: the free plan single automation and lack of sequences means any creator who wants a real welcome sequence or funnel has to upgrade to Creator immediately, the free plan generous subscriber cap does not help if you need automation on day one. The October 2025 price hike also makes Creator Pro a real jump for creators scaling past a few thousand subscribers.
  • MailerLite: the 2026 free-plan tightening to 250 subscribers is a real drop from what the free tier used to offer, so a creator starting from zero will hit the paywall much faster than on Kit. There is also no path to monetize your list without adding a separate tool.

FAQ

Is Kit or MailerLite better for creators?

Kit is built specifically for creators selling digital products, paid newsletters, and subscriptions, with built-in monetization (Tip Jars, paid subscriptions, product sales) and a creator recommendation network that MailerLite does not offer. MailerLite is the cheaper, more general-purpose email tool, stronger drag-and-drop design and a lower price at low subscriber counts, but no built-in monetization or creator network. If you are actively selling something to your list, Kit monetization features usually pay for the price gap. If you just need to send a clean-looking newsletter as cheaply as possible, MailerLite is the better value.

How much do Kit and MailerLite cost?

Kit Newsletter plan is free for up to 10,000 subscribers with 1 automation. Kit Creator starts at 39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers (33 dollars per month billed annually), scaling to 139 dollars per month at 10,000 subscribers. Kit Creator Pro starts at 79 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers (66 dollars per month annually). MailerLite free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails per month. MailerLite Comfort starts at 19 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers, and Power starts at 39 dollars per month for 1,000 subscribers.

Which is cheaper for a small list?

MailerLite is cheaper at every subscriber tier list price to list price: Comfort at 1,000 subscribers is 19 dollars per month versus Kit Creator 39 dollars per month. But Kit free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, far more generous than MailerLite free plan cap of 250 subscribers. For a creator under 10,000 subscribers who does not need automations yet, Kit free plan can beat MailerLite cheapest paid tier on cost.
