pCloud Free Storage: How the 10GB Limit Actually Works (2026)

Last updated October 9, 2026 · ~10 min read · by StackHonest

Disclosure: the Zoho WorkDrive link marked below sits in an affiliate slot on this site, but no Zoho affiliate code is live here yet, so that link earns exactly $0 today — it is a placeholder, not a payday. pCloud, Google, Dropbox and Microsoft have no affiliate relationship with this site at all, so every link to them is a plain editorial citation to the page I read the numbers off. Nothing here is sponsored, no vendor saw this before it went live, and this site has collected no affiliate revenue to date.

Short answer: pCloud advertises a free plan "Up to 10 GB," and that figure is real -- but it is not what lands in your account the moment you sign up. pCloud's own help center puts registration at about 2GB, then breaks the rest into five 1GB rewards for completing a beginner checklist and up to 3GB more for referring friends, capping the total at 10GB. The paid Premium subscription is $4.99/month for 500GB, and -- unusually -- paying annually buys no discount at all: $59.88/year is exactly 12 times $4.99. The one-time Lifetime plans are a different story: two loads of pCloud's own pricing page minutes apart on October 9, 2026 returned two different sale prices for the same tier, so this article reports both instead of picking one. Every figure below was read off pCloud's own pages the same day.

The free plan, in pCloud's own words

pCloud's help center (help.pcloud.com/article/plan-details) lists the entry tier plainly:

What the page saysVerbatim

Shared link traffic"Shared Link Traffic: 50 GB per month"
Upload traffic"Upload Traffic: Up to twice your storage space within 30 days"

"Up to" is the operative phrase, and a second help article (help.pcloud.com/article/referral-program) spells out exactly how you get from your starting balance to the full 10GB. It is a checklist, not a lump sum.

How the 10GB free limit actually breaks down

Quoting the referral-program help article directly, because the breakdown matters more than the total: "In addition to the referral program, you can unlock up to 10GB of free space with your basic plan by completing the following: 1. Complete the Beginner's tutorial (each step unlocks 1GB): Verify your email address, Upload a file to pCloud, Download and log in to the pCloud Drive desktop application, Download and log in to the pCloud mobile application, Enable the Automatic Upload feature in the pCloud mobile application. 2. Participate in the Invite Friends program -- each successful friend invitation unlocks an additional 1GB of free space." A separate section of the same help center caps that referral bonus at 3GB: "you can earn up to 3GB of extra free storage by inviting friends."

StepStorage unlocked

The arithmetic holds: 2 + 5 (five 1GB tutorial steps) + 3 (capped referral bonus) = 10. Referrals from the same IP are capped at 10 successfully registered users, each invite needs the friend to verify their email to count, and the help center adds a sweetener unrelated to the storage itself: every successful referral also enters you in a monthly drawing for a Premium Lifetime plan (500GB), with "each user can win only once." None of that is storage you can bank on -- it is a prize draw layered on top of the referral program, and this article treats it as exactly that, not as expected value.

Two traffic ceilings matter once you are actually using the free tier, both from the same help article: 50GB of shared-link traffic a month -- the bandwidth consumed when other people view or download files you have shared publicly -- and upload traffic capped at roughly twice your storage allowance within any 30-day window. At the full 10GB that is about 20GB you can upload in a rolling month before pCloud throttles further uploads, which matters if you are using the free tier as a one-time migration dump rather than steady-state backup.

Subscription pricing: no annual discount, which is the finding

pcloud.com's pricing page (pcloud.com/cloud-storage-pricing-plans.html) splits into three tabs: Monthly, Annual and Lifetime. Reading the Monthly tab directly on October 9, 2026:

Do the multiplication and there is no gap: $4.99 × 12 = $59.88. $9.99 × 12 = $119.88. $29.99 × 12 = $359.88. Every single annual total printed on the page is exactly twelve times the monthly rate, to the cent. That is worth naming because it runs against the near-universal pattern on this site's other reviews, where annual billing buys a real discount over month-to-month (Bitwarden, Zoho Sign, Zoho CRM and Dropbox Sign all do it). pCloud's Monthly and Annual subscription tabs charge identically; the only place a discount shows up on this page at all is the Lifetime tab, covered next.

Lifetime plan economics: the vendor's own page could not agree with itself

The Lifetime tab is where pCloud's pitch gets interesting -- pay once, keep the storage forever, no subscription. It is also where this article hit a real data-integrity problem worth documenting in detail rather than smoothing over.

Two separate fetches of the identical URL, minutes apart on October 9, 2026, returned two different price pairs for the same three Lifetime tiers:

Both pairs are real: they do not come from a cache or a stale mirror -- search engines' own indexed snippets of the exact same pricing page independently reproduce both sets of numbers, meaning pCloud's page has served at least two different "current" Lifetime sale prices within the same general window, not a one-time glitch on this article's end. There is no way to tell from the outside whether this is an A/B price test, a session-dependent promo, or a simple bug -- and this article is not going to guess. No single Lifetime price appears anywhere else on this page as "the" current rate, because there is no way to verify which one a given reader will actually be shown.

What both fetches agree on: the storage and traffic figures per tier (500GB/1,000GB traffic, 2TB/4TB traffic, 10TB/20TB traffic match the subscription tiers' allowances), a 14-day money-back guarantee, "no setup fees or other hidden charges," and that Lifetime purchases can later be upgraded to a larger Lifetime plan. If you are pricing a Lifetime purchase, treat whatever number your own browser shows you as provisional and reload the page before you pay -- this article's own experience says it may not match the last tab you had open.

Encryption add-on cost: pCloud's own two pages disagree

pCloud's standard encryption (TLS/SSL in transit, 256-bit AES at rest) is included on every plan, free and paid, with pCloud holding the keys server-side. Real end-to-end, zero-knowledge encryption -- a separate "Crypto Folder" that pCloud says it cannot read -- is a paid add-on called pCloud Encryption, and it is sold separately from storage. Two of pCloud's own pages quote different prices for it:

$3.99 × 12 is $47.88. The live page says $49.99. That is a $2.11-a-year gap between pCloud's own support documentation and its own live pricing page, read the same day. Neither figure is invented here -- both are quoted verbatim from pCloud's own properties, and this article is not resolving the discrepancy on the vendor's behalf. The support article also states plainly that Business plans get Crypto included free, and that pricing "depends on your billing country" with GBP/EUR-plus-VAT variants for the UK and EU.

One more thing worth naming plainly, because it is a security product and the stakes are different from a pricing quibble: researchers at ETH Zurich published attacks against the Crypto Folder in 2024. Multiple independent reviewers report that, as of their own review dates, pCloud had not announced a public fix. This article did not independently verify pCloud's current cryptographic posture, found no dated statement from pCloud addressing the research, and is not claiming the issue is fixed, unfixed, or irrelevant to today's implementation. If zero-knowledge encryption is the reason you are considering pCloud, that history is worth reading directly before paying for the add-on.

When pCloud free is the right answer, and when it is not

  1. You want to try the ecosystem before paying anything. ~2GB instantly, growing to 10GB in about ten minutes of real tutorial steps. Fine for testing sync and sharing before committing.
  2. You want the single largest instant free tier. Google Drive's 15GB, handed over with zero steps, beats pCloud's 10GB-after-effort outright. Skip pCloud free for this specific goal.
  3. You want to pay once and be done with subscriptions. pCloud's Lifetime tiers are the draw -- just reload the pricing page before you buy, since this article caught it serving two different sale prices for the same tier on the same day.
  4. You want true zero-knowledge encryption and no history of researchers finding holes in it. pCloud Crypto's 2024 ETH Zurich findings are unresolved in the public record this article could find. Look elsewhere if that is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.
  5. You are a team of 3+ that needs shared folders and admin controls, not personal backup. Zoho WorkDrive's published per-seat pricing (minimum 3 seats) is built for that; pCloud and Google Drive are built for individuals.

Verdict

pCloud's "up to 10GB free" claim is accurate but earned, not given -- about 2GB at signup, five 1GB tutorial rewards, and up to 3GB in capped referrals. If an instant, no-effort free tier is what you actually want, Google Drive's 15GB beats it without any of the steps. Where pCloud gets genuinely interesting is the Lifetime option for people tired of subscriptions -- but this article caught its own pricing page serving two different sale prices for the same tier within minutes, so verify the number you are actually being shown before paying. The Crypto encryption add-on has a real, unresolved security history worth reading before you buy it specifically for its zero-knowledge promise, and its own price does not even agree between two of pCloud's own pages. None of that makes pCloud a bad product; it makes it a product whose own marketing pages need a second look before you commit money to them.

FAQ

Does pCloud really give 10GB of free storage?

Up to 10GB, built in stages, not handed over at signup. pCloud's own help center lists the Basic free plan as "Storage: Up to 10 GB," and a companion article spells out how you get there: about 2GB on registration, five 1GB unlocks for completing the Beginner's tutorial (verify email, upload a file, install the desktop app, install the mobile app, turn on Automatic Upload), and up to 3GB more through the Invite Friends referral program. 2 + 5 + 3 = 10. Skip the tutorial and the referrals and you keep roughly 2GB, not 10.

What are pCloud's paid subscription prices in 2026?

Read off pcloud.com's own Monthly subscription tab on October 9, 2026: Premium (500GB) is $4.99 a month, Premium Plus (2TB) is $9.99 a month, and Ultra (10TB) is $29.99 a month. The page prints an annual total next to each figure -- $59.88, $119.88 and $359.88 -- and those are exactly 12 times the monthly rate with no discount applied. pCloud's subscription tier charges the same per month whether you pay monthly or annually.

Why does pCloud's Lifetime price keep changing?

It is not one number. Two separate loads of pCloud's own pricing page, minutes apart on October 9, 2026, returned two different discounted-from-regular price pairs for the identical Premium 500GB Lifetime tier: $199 from $299 on one load, versus $219 from $279 on the other -- and the same split pattern repeated for Premium Plus 2TB and Ultra 10TB. Both pairs also surface independently in search engines' own cached snippets of the same page. No single Lifetime price is stated anywhere in this article as the current one.

How much does pCloud Encryption (Crypto) cost?

pCloud's own support article states the add-on costs 3.99 USD per month on an annual subscription or 4.99 USD per month on a monthly subscription, plus a $150 one-time Lifetime option, free only on Business plans. The live pcloud.com/crypto.html page, read the same day, prints a different annual figure: $49.99 a year -- which works out to $4.16 a month, not $3.99. Both are pCloud's own pages; they do not agree.

Is pCloud Crypto actually zero-knowledge, and is it safe?

pCloud markets Crypto as client-side, zero-knowledge encryption it says it cannot decrypt. What this article will not do is vouch for its security track record: researchers at ETH Zurich published attacks against the Crypto Folder in 2024, and no dated vendor statement confirming a fix was found while researching this article. The claim is reported as an open question, not resolved either way.

How does pCloud's free plan compare to Google Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive?

By ceiling, not by ease of reaching it. pCloud tops out at 10GB free but needs five tutorial steps plus referrals to get there. Google Drive gives every account 15GB free instantly, shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos. Microsoft OneDrive gives 5GB free instantly. Dropbox Basic gives just 2GB free instantly, expandable to a 16GB referral ceiling -- and Dropbox's own current pricing page does not list the free Basic plan among its cards at all, the same pattern this site found earlier on Dropbox Sign's free e-signature tier.
