How to Track Affiliate Link Clicks and Conversions in 2026 (Without an Enterprise Tracker)

Last updated October 2026 · ~8 min read · by Juan

Affiliate disclosure: some links below are affiliate links. If you sign up through them I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Every opinion here comes from checking vendor pricing pages directly, not from marketing copy.

Short answer: if you run a few affiliate programs from a blog or a small list, free UTM parameters plus the conversion dashboard each affiliate network already gives you is enough -- a paid tracker is not needed yet. If paid ad traffic runs across multiple campaigns and the goal is knowing in real time which specific ad, landing page and offer combination is converting, a dedicated tracker earns its cost: ClickMagick starts at $66/mo billed annually (clickmagick.com), Voluum starts around $119/mo billed annually. GetResponse click tracking, despite the name, measures clicks inside emails sent through GetResponse -- it does not track or attribute affiliate link clicks on a blog page to a third-party sale. All pricing verified directly against vendor pages October 3, 2026.

The mismatch this article exists to fix

A roundup brief that lands in this content queue every few weeks asks for AI-enhanced affiliate tracking and names GetResponse as the primary tool. That premise does not hold up against GetResponse own pricing page. GetResponse click tracking is a feature of its email marketing product: it shows which link inside an email campaign got clicked, by whom, and how many times. It has no redirect-and-attribute mechanism for a visitor clicking an affiliate link embedded in a blog post, landing on a third-party checkout page, where a sale happens there. Those are two unrelated jobs that happen to share the word tracking.

Option 1: free, manual, good enough for low-to-moderate volume

If the plan is promoting one or two affiliate programs from organic content or a small list, this is the right starting point and it costs nothing:

  • Build each affiliate link with UTM parameters using Google free Campaign URL Builder, tagging source/medium/campaign per page or email to track origin.
  • Route the tagged link through a free shortener that logs click counts, or skip the shortener and rely on the affiliate network own click log if it has one.
  • Check each affiliate network own dashboard (Impact, ShareASale, CJ, PartnerStack, Systeme.io affiliate portal, etc. all show their own click and conversion data) and log the numbers into a spreadsheet weekly.

What this setup will not give: cross-network deduplication, automated bot filtering, or a single dashboard that computes ROI per campaign automatically. For a solo operator with a handful of programs and no paid ad spend, that is a trade worth making to avoid a $66-plus monthly bill.

Which one to actually pick

If no paid ad traffic currently runs to affiliate links, skip both paid trackers and use Option 1. If paid ads are starting up and the goal is knowing which specific ad/landing-page/offer combination is profitable, ClickMagick $66/mo Starter tier is the cheaper entry point and is purpose-built for affiliate marketers specifically. Voluum earns its higher price once enough simultaneous campaigns and event volume exist that its AI traffic-distribution and multi-network dashboarding genuinely save time -- for a single-person operation running one or two offers, that threshold is further out than the marketing copy implies.

FAQ

Does GetResponse track affiliate link clicks and conversions?

Not the way the question implies. GetResponse click tracking (getresponse.com/pricing, verified October 3, 2026) measures clicks on links inside emails sent through GetResponse itself -- it has no mechanism to track a visitor clicking an affiliate link on a blog post, through a redirect, to a third-party checkout page, and attribute a resulting sale back to that click. That is a different product category: dedicated ad/affiliate trackers like ClickMagick or Voluum, or a manual UTM-plus-spreadsheet setup. GetResponse earns a place on this site for email automation, not for this.

Is there a free way to track affiliate link clicks?

Yes, for solo affiliates doing low-to-moderate volume. Build affiliate links with UTM parameters (Google Campaign URL Builder is free), route them through a free link shortener that logs click counts, and log which link went where in a spreadsheet. Click counts become visible and can be cross-referenced against affiliate network dashboards (Impact, ShareASale, etc. all show their own conversion data) manually. It does not give cross-network dedupe, bot filtering, or automated ROI math -- that is what paid trackers sell.

Is a dedicated tracker needed when just starting out with one or two affiliate programs?

No. A dedicated tracker like ClickMagick or Voluum earns its monthly cost when paid ad traffic is running across multiple campaigns and the goal is knowing, in real time, which specific ad/landing-page/offer combination is actually converting so losers can be killed and winners scaled. If affiliate traffic comes from organic blog content or a small email list promoting one or two programs, UTM parameters plus the conversion dashboard each affiliate network already gives is enough -- paying $66-$291/month for infrastructure that automation will not get used does not make sense yet.
