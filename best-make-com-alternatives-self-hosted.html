Short answer: Make.com cannot be self-hosted at any price -- it's cloud-only. If you want Make-style visual automation on your own server, n8n Community Edition is the best alternative for most people: free software, unlimited executions, and it runs on a $5-7/month VPS. Activepieces is the friendlier runner-up (open source, cloud from $16/month Plus billed yearly), and Huginn is the free-forever tinkerer's option if you don't need a visual editor. The 30-second answer

First, the disqualifier: there is no self-hosted Make. Anyone searching this phrase wants one of two things -- flat-cost executions or data on their own box. For both, n8n Community Edition on a cheap VPS is the answer: unlimited runs, 1,000+ integrations, code steps when you need them. Choose Activepieces instead if n8n's node editor feels too technical and you want something closer to Make's feel. Stay on Make if your volume fits its free 1,000 credits or the ~$12/mo Core tier -- migration costs a weekend you may not need to spend.

Why people leave Make (and the metering math)

Make is genuinely good -- I said so in my n8n vs Make comparison and it's still my pick for non-technical folks. The problem is the meter. Per make.com/en/pricing this week: Free is 1,000 credits/month with a 15-minute minimum interval between runs; Core is $12/month for 10,000 credits; Pro $21; Teams $38 (all priced at the 10k-credit level, billed annually). Every operation in a scenario burns credits, so a busy lead-capture flow -- the kind I break down in my lead-gen automation guide -- can chew through 10,000 credits in a week. When that happens, your bill scales with your success. Self-hosting swaps that meter for one flat server bill.

Option by option

1. n8n Community Edition -- the default answer

n8n's self-hosted Community Edition is free software with no execution caps: the only bill is the server, and a $5-7/month VPS handles a one-person automation load easily (full hosting rundown, including Hostinger's n8n-template VPS from $6.49/month, in my n8n hosting options guide). You get the visual editor, 1,000+ integrations, JavaScript/Python code steps, and webhook triggers with no minimum-interval nonsense -- Make's free tier won't run a scenario more than once every 15 minutes; a self-hosted n8n webhook fires instantly.

Honest catches: the node-graph editor is a step more technical than Make's -- data mapping between nodes trips up beginners for the first week. And "free software" still means you're the sysadmin: updates, backups, and the occasional 2am container restart are yours. One pricing note for anyone comparing against n8n's own cloud: n8n Cloud now prices in euros -- Starter 20€/month and Pro 50€/month billed annually per n8n.io/pricing this week -- so old dollar quotes you'll see around the web are stale.

2. Activepieces -- the most Make-like of the bunch

Activepieces is open source at the core with the friendliest editor in the self-hosted field -- if your team liked Make's drag-and-drop, this is the smallest retraining cost. Per activepieces.com/pricing this week: Free forever (no card, daily credit refresh), Plus at $16/month flat billed yearly, Team at $166/month flat, and -- unusually -- every plan runs on their cloud or your own servers at the same price. One credit covers a whole run regardless of step count, which is a friendlier meter than Make's per-operation billing.

Honest catches: 760+ integrations is solid but well short of n8n's list and a fraction of Make's 3,000+, so check your specific apps before migrating. And note the self-hosted paid tiers mean the "free self-host" story only covers Free-plan features -- the advanced bits (embedding, white-label, some team features) stay behind the paid plans even on your own hardware.

3. Huginn -- free forever, if you're willing to work for it

Huginn is the old-guard open-source option (MIT license, around since 2013): "agents" that watch, scrape, and react on your behalf, fully free with no paid tier at all. It's the right pick for a tinkerer who wants zero license cost and total control.

Honest catches: there's no visual workflow editor -- you configure agents through forms and JSON, and modern SaaS integrations mostly mean writing your own webhook/API glue. Coming from Make, this is a parachute jump, not a lateral move. I'd only send you here if the words "I'll just read the source" sound like fun.

The dark horse: just staying on Make

Recommending the incumbent is boring, but honesty first: if your monthly usage fits inside 1,000 free credits -- or the ~$12 Core tier covers you -- self-hosting saves you nothing and costs you real maintenance hours. The switch pays off at volume, for instant webhook triggers, or when data can't leave your infrastructure. Run your last three months of Make usage before you migrate; plenty of people port everything to a VPS to save $12 a month and inherit a part-time sysadmin job.

FAQ

What is the best self-hosted alternative to Make.com in 2026?

n8n Community Edition: free software, unlimited executions, 1,000+ integrations, runs on a $5-7/month VPS. Activepieces is the friendlier runner-up with a more Make-like editor.

Is self-hosting actually cheaper than Make?

Only past Make's metering. Free covers 1,000 credits/month and Core is about $12/month for 10,000 -- below that, stay put. Above it, a flat $6 VPS running n8n wins every month.

Can I self-host Make.com itself?

No -- Make has no self-hosted version at any price. Switching tools is the only way to get automation on your own hardware.
