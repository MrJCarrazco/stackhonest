Vultr's Cheapest VPS (2026): $2.50/mo Is IPv6-Only — $3.50 Is the Real Floor

Last updated October 5, 2026 · ~10 min read · by Juan

Affiliate disclosure: the Hostinger links on this page are affiliate links — if you sign up through one I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Vultr, DigitalOcean and Hetzner have no affiliate relationship with this site, so every link to those vendors is a plain editorial citation to the page I read the numbers off. Nothing here is sponsored, and no vendor saw this before it went live.

Short answer: the cheapest instance on Vultr's pricing page is $2.50/month ($0.004/hour) for 1 vCPU, 0.5 GB of memory, 10 GB of storage and 0.5 TB of bandwidth — but that row is badged "IPv6 Only", so it has no public IPv4 address. The cheapest plan with identical specs and an IPv4 address is $3.50/month ($0.005/hour). For most people that $3.50 figure, not $2.50, is the real entry price. Everything below was read directly off each vendor's own pricing page on October 5, 2026.

The premise I had to correct: "low single digits per month" hides an asterisk

The brief that sent me to this page said Vultr's lowest-cost cloud compute instance "starts in the low single digits per month with hourly billing." Both halves are technically true and the sentence is still misleading, because the number everyone quotes is attached to a plan most people cannot actually use as-is.

  • Correct: $2.50/mo exists, and billing is hourly. Vultr's Regular Performance table lists both a monthly and an hourly price for every row, and the bottom row is $2.50/mo or $0.004/hr.
  • Omitted: that row has no IPv4 address. Vultr prints a small "IPv6 Only" badge next to the $0.004/hr price. The very next row is the same 1 vCPU / 0.5 GB / 10 GB / 0.5 TB machine at $3.50/mo ($0.005/hr) — the difference between the two is the address, and it costs $1.00/month.
  • Why that matters practically: if your home ISP, your office network, a payment provider's webhook sender or a client's firewall is IPv4-only, an IPv6-only box is unreachable from it. You can work around it with a proxy, but "work around it" is not what people mean when they quote a $2.50 VPS.
  • The brief also listed "IPv4" under hidden costs. That framing does not match the page: Vultr publishes no standalone per-IPv4-address fee on vultr.com/pricing. The only IPv4 price signal on that page is the $1.00/month gap between those two plans. I am not going to invent a line item that the vendor does not print.

The add-ons that are not in the sticker price

All four figures below come from Vultr's own pricing FAQ and add-on sections, October 5 2026:

  • Automatic backups are not a flat fee — they are a percentage. Vultr's FAQ: "An instance with automatic backups enabled will have a 20% higher base monthly/hourly fee." So backups cost $0.50/mo on the $2.50 plan, $0.70 on the $3.50 plan, and $8.00/mo on a $40 plan. Budget this as a multiplier, not a line item.
  • Stored snapshots: $0.05/GB per month, with Vultr adding "pricing subject to change" in the same answer. A 10 GB snapshot of the entry instance is $0.50/month, which is another 20% on top of a $2.50 box.
  • DDoS protection: $10/month per instance, adding 10 Gbps of mitigation capacity. On the entry plan that is four times the cost of the server.
  • Block storage: $1.00/month per 10 GB of NVMe. Useful if you want a small instance with a bigger disk rather than paying for a larger plan you do not need the CPU for.
  • Windows licences are not included. Vultr's FAQ states plainly that the monthly price excludes a Windows licence and that the extra cost varies by compute plan. No figure is published on the pricing page, so none appears here.
  • Prices are not identical in every region. Vultr's FAQ says certain global data-centre locations "may have different per hour and per month pricing," and that the current location pricing lives in the control panel. So the $2.50 you read on the marketing page is not a guarantee in every city.

Stacked honestly: a $2.50 instance with automatic backups and a 10 GB snapshot is $3.50/month before you have added an IPv4 address. With the address it is $4.20. The headline number is real; it is just not what a working box costs.

When a cheap cloud instance is the wrong shape entirely

The $2.50–$5.00 cloud tier is priced per hour and sized for small, stateless, low-memory jobs. If what you actually want is a box to self-host an application stack on — the single most common reason people go VPS shopping — the binding constraint is RAM, and the cheap cloud tier runs out of it before it runs out of anything else.

For that job the fixed-term shared-hosting providers win on memory per dollar, and Hostinger's VPS line is the one I keep coming back to because it bundles the one-click application templates. From hostinger.com/vps-hosting, October 5 2026:

Verdict

The cheapest usable Vultr VPS is $3.50/month, not $2.50. The $2.50 row is real but IPv6-only; the dollar more buys the IPv4 address most setups need. Add automatic backups (+20%) and a snapshot ($0.05/GB/mo) and a working entry box is around $4.20/month. It is genuinely the lowest sticker price of the three providers I could price — just never the number people repeat.

If raw memory per dollar is what you are shopping for rather than the lowest possible monthly minimum, Hostinger's KVM tiers give you 4–32 GB of RAM at a promotional rate, with the catch that you pay the term upfront and the renewal is roughly 85% higher. Read the renewal line on the card before the promo price convinces you.

FAQ

How much is the cheapest Vultr VPS per month?

$2.50 per month, or $0.004 per hour, for 1 vCPU, 0.5 GB of memory, 10 GB of storage and 0.5 TB of bandwidth on Vultr's Regular Performance Cloud Compute line. That row carries an "IPv6 Only" badge on vultr.com/pricing, meaning the instance has no public IPv4 address. The cheapest plan with the same specs and an IPv4 address is $3.50/mo ($0.005/hr). Both figures were read off Vultr's pricing page on October 5 2026.

Does Vultr's $2.50 plan come with an IPv4 address?

No. The $2.50/mo row is marked "IPv6 Only" on the pricing page. If anything needs to reach your server over IPv4 -- an office network without IPv6, a webhook sender that is IPv4-only, or your own ISP -- you want the $3.50/mo row instead. Vultr does not publish a standalone per-IPv4-address price on its pricing page; the only number the page gives you is the $1.00/month difference between those two otherwise identical plans.

What is Vultr's bandwidth overage rate?

$0.01 per GB for bandwidth used beyond your plan's quota, per Vultr's own pricing FAQ. On the entry plans the quota is 0.5 TB, so exceeding it by 500 GB adds $5.00 -- double the price of the $2.50 instance itself. Bandwidth quota scales with plan size: 1 TB at $5/mo, 2 TB at $10/mo, 3 TB at $15 and $20, up to 15 TB at the $640/mo tier.

How much do Vultr backups and snapshots cost?

They are priced two different ways. Automatic backups are not a flat fee: Vultr's FAQ states an instance with automatic backups enabled has a 20% higher base monthly/hourly fee, so backups on the $2.50 plan cost $0.50/mo and on a $40 plan cost $8/mo. Stored snapshots are metered separately at $0.05/GB per month, which Vultr notes is subject to change. DDoS protection is a further $10/mo per instance.

Is Vultr cheaper than DigitalOcean?

At the very bottom, yes, but the gap is small and the specs differ. Vultr's entry is $2.50/mo IPv6-only or $3.50/mo with IPv4 (0.5 GB RAM, 10 GB disk, 0.5 TB transfer). DigitalOcean's cheapest Basic Droplet is $4.00/mo for 512 MiB RAM, 10 GiB SSD and 500 GiB transfer. Both charge $0.01 per GB of overage. DigitalOcean moved to per-second billing effective January 1 2026 with a 60-second minimum; Vultr bills hourly. Both pages verified October 5 2026.

Is a $2.50 VPS enough to run anything real?

0.5 GB of RAM is enough for a static site, a small reverse proxy, a Tailscale or WireGuard node, a cron runner or a tiny bot. It is not enough for most self-hosted app stacks: n8n's own documentation asks for more, and anything running a database plus an application container on half a gigabyte will swap or get OOM-killed. If you are sizing a box for self-hosted automation specifically, compare RAM per dollar rather than the headline monthly price -- Hostinger's KVM 1 advertises 4 GB at a promotional $6.49/mo, which is eight times the memory for under twice the money, at the cost of paying the term upfront.
