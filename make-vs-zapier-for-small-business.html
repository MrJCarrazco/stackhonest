Make vs Zapier for Small Business (2026): Which One Actually Fits Your Budget

Last updated September 2026 · ~8 min read · by Juan

Affiliate disclosure: some links below are affiliate links. If you sign up through them I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Every opinion here comes from actually running these tools -- I will tell you when something is not worth your money.

Short answer: for most small businesses on a real budget, Make gives you more room to grow before you have to pay, and more room again once you do -- its free plan is 1,000 operations a month versus Zapier's 100 tasks, and its entry paid tier is roughly a third of Zapier's price for more than 10x the volume. Zapier still wins on day-one simplicity and app coverage, so if you're automating your very first workflow and just want the least friction, start there. If you're already running a handful of automations for the business, Make is where the math works out.

The 30-second answer

Pick Make if you're running (or planning to run) more than 2-3 automations, want visual branching without paying developer rates, and care about the monthly bill. Pick Zapier if this is your first automation tool ever, you need an app that's only in Zapier's directory, or your workflows are genuinely simple 2-step triggers.

Ease of use: Zapier still wins the first afternoon

Zapier's editor is linear and forgiving: trigger, then action, then another action. If you've never built an automation, you'll have your first working Zap in ten minutes, and the error messages actually tell you what broke. Its app directory is also the largest in the industry -- if a tool exists, Zapier probably already connects to it natively.

Make trades some of that friction for power. It's a visual canvas -- you place modules and draw connections, and you can build branches, routers, and loops that would take several separate Zaps to replicate. That flexibility is genuinely useful once your business has more than one "if this, then one of several things" situation (a new order routes differently depending on product type, a lead routes differently depending on source). The tradeoff is a steeper first hour: the canvas metaphor takes some getting used to if you've only ever seen linear tools.

AI features: both added them, neither is a specialist

Both platforms have bolted on AI features -- Make has AI app modules and an AI agent builder in beta; Zapier has "Zapier AI" tools for building Zaps from prompts and AI-powered actions inside workflows. Neither is built AI-first the way n8n is, with native agent nodes, memory, and tool-calling as core primitives rather than add-ons. If AI agents are the actual point of your automation (not just triggering off an AI output), n8n is the stronger pick -- see my n8n vs Zapier and n8n vs Make breakdowns for that comparison. For most small-business use cases here -- routing leads, syncing spreadsheets, sending notifications, updating a CRM -- either Make or Zapier's built-in AI helpers are enough.

Where each one breaks (first-hand scars)

Zapier's failure modes

  • The free plan is a demo, not a tool. 100 tasks and a 2-step cap means you'll outgrow it in the first week of real use, not the first month.
  • Costs scale fast with complexity. Every extra step in a Zap is another task, every run. A busy multi-step Zap can burn a monthly allowance in days.
  • Complex branching gets awkward -- Paths help, but true multi-condition routing often means chaining several Zaps together with storage steps in between.

Verdict by business type

  • Brand new to automation, one simple workflow: Zapier free plan. Get something working today, worry about cost later.
  • Running 3+ automations or planning to: Make. The free and Core tiers carry real volume before you pay meaningful money.
  • Watching the automation line item on a tight budget: Make, decisively -- the entry paid tier is a third of Zapier's price for more than 10x the operations.
  • Need an app that's only in one directory: Whichever platform has it -- check both app lists before deciding on price alone.

FAQ

Is Make cheaper than Zapier for a small business?

Usually, yes. Make's free plan gives 1,000 operations a month and its entry paid tier (~$9-10.59/mo) gives 10,000 operations. Zapier's free plan caps at 100 tasks and only allows 2-step Zaps; its entry paid tier (Professional, ~$29.99/mo) gives 750 tasks. For the same multi-step workflow, Make almost always stretches further per dollar.

Is Zapier easier to use than Make for a small business owner?

Zapier is still the simplest on-ramp -- linear trigger-then-action Zaps and the largest app directory. Make's visual canvas is more powerful (branches, routers, loops) but has a real learning curve if you've never seen a flowchart-style builder before.

Can I run my small business automation for free?

Yes, up to a point. Make's free tier (1,000 ops/mo, 2 active scenarios) fits a genuinely small operation. Zapier's free tier (100 tasks/mo, 2-step Zaps only) is really a test drive, not a working setup. For zero-dollar automation at real volume, self-hosted n8n is the better fit than either -- see my n8n vs Zapier breakdown.
