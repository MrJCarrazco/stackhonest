Best AI Tools for E-Commerce Product Descriptions at Scale (2026)

Short answer: if you are already on Shopify, start with Shopify Magic -- it is free, built into every plan, and writes a usable first-draft product description in the admin with zero extra subscription. If you need bulk generation across hundreds of SKUs at once, a dedicated catalog app (credit-metered per description) beats general-purpose AI writers like Koala or Jasper, which are priced and built for blog articles, not thousands of short product listings. Koala AI (Starter $25/mo) works fine for a small catalog (under roughly 150-300 SKUs a month, depending on length) if you are also using it for blog content, but it is not purpose-built for catalog-scale product copy the way a dedicated bulk-description app is. Why "best AI writer" and "best product-description tool" are not the same question

Most "best AI tools for product descriptions" roundups just list general-purpose AI writers (Jasper, Copy.ai, Koala) and call it a day. That is a mismatch. Those tools are priced and metered around words or credits, sized for blog articles that run 1,000-2,500 words each. A product description is 50-200 words. If you are writing 500 of them a month, you are not writing 500 "articles" -- you are doing bulk catalog work, and the tools built for that bill per-product or per-credit at a completely different scale than a blog writer's word pool.

Shopify Magic -- the free option if you are already on Shopify

If your store runs on Shopify, Shopify Magic is included on every plan (Basic and up) at no extra cost -- it drafts a product description from a product title and a few bullet points, right inside the product-edit screen. It will not batch-process your entire catalog in one click and the output needs the same human truth-pass any AI draft does, but for a store adding products one at a time, it is genuinely $0 marginal cost if you are already paying for Shopify (plans run from $29/mo as of 2026 pricing, after any new-store promo pricing expires).

Bulk catalog apps: the actual right tool for hundreds of SKUs

For stores that need to generate or rewrite descriptions across a large catalog in one pass, dedicated Shopify App Store tools built specifically for this exist and price accordingly -- credit-metered per description rather than per word. A representative example: a free tier covering up to 120 manually-entered descriptions, then paid tiers starting around $15/mo for roughly 2,500 generated descriptions a month, scaling to $30/mo (about 12,000) and $100/mo (about 120,000). That is the pricing shape to look for -- how many products does this plan cover, not how many words.

Pay-as-you-go catalog tools also exist, charging a flat per-product rate (commonly well under $1 per product) with no subscription at all -- worth considering if your catalog work is a one-time bulk job rather than an ongoing monthly need.

Koala AI -- fine for small catalogs, not built for bulk

I have reviewed Koala Writer in depth for blog-article production, where it is genuinely strong. For product descriptions specifically: Koala's entry Starter plan is $25/month for 45,000 KoalaWriter words and 500 platform credits (verified koala.sh/pricing, October 3, 2026 -- the old $9/mo Essentials tier is gone). At 150-300 words per product description, that is roughly 150-300 descriptions a month on Starter, which is workable for a boutique store adding new SKUs steadily, but it is not priced or positioned for a one-time 2,000-SKU catalog migration the way a dedicated bulk app is. If you are already paying for Koala for blog content, using the same subscription for occasional product copy is reasonable; buying it specifically for catalog work usually is not the cheapest path.

Copy.ai and Hypotenuse AI -- priced for marketing teams, not catalogs

Copy.ai current pricing (copy.ai/prices, verified October 2026) runs a Chat plan at $29/mo ($24/mo billed annually) with no workflow credits included, then an Agents tier at $249/mo for up to 10 seats and 10,000 workflow credits -- squarely enterprise/team pricing, not a solo-store tool. Hypotenuse AI Entry tier runs about $29/mo ($19/mo annual) for 50,000 words and one seat, with its e-commerce-specific plans quote-based rather than published -- contact sales if bulk catalog work is genuinely your use case, but expect pricing scaled for agencies and larger stores, not a weekend side-hustle shop.

What I will not claim

No vendor publishes an audited benchmark showing AI-written product descriptions convert better than human-written ones, and none of the tools above publish a controlled A/B test of their own output against a baseline. Any percentage-lift figure circulating for AI product copy traces back to vendor marketing, not independent testing -- this article sticks to pricing and catalog-scale fit, which are the parts you can actually verify before you pay.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to generate AI product descriptions for Shopify?

Shopify Magic is free and built into every Shopify plan -- it writes product descriptions right in the admin with no separate subscription. If you are already paying for Shopify (Basic from $29/mo, after any introductory promo), that is $0 marginal cost before you need anything fancier.

Does Koala AI cap the number of product descriptions you can generate?

No -- Koala meters by words and platform credits, not a fixed article count. Starter ($25/mo) includes 45,000 KoalaWriter words per month, which works out to roughly 150-300 short product descriptions (150-300 words each) depending on length, not a hard SKU cap. It is built for blog-style SEO articles, not bulk catalog generation, so it is a worse fit than tools built specifically for product copy at volume.

What is the best AI tool for generating hundreds of product descriptions at once?

Dedicated Shopify apps built for bulk catalog work (credit-metered per description, for example a $15/mo tier covering roughly 2,500 descriptions) or pay-as-you-go catalog tools priced per product are a better fit than general-purpose writers like Koala or Jasper, which charge by word/credit pools sized for blog content, not thousands of short product listings.
