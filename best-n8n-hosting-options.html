Option by option

1. Raw VPS with an n8n template -- the value pick

This is how I run mine. Hostinger sells VPS plans with n8n pre-installed via an Ubuntu 24.04 template: KVM 1 at $6.49/month, KVM 2 at $8.99, KVM 4 at $12.99, KVM 8 at $25.99 -- all with unlimited workflows, unlimited concurrent executions, community nodes, and even a queue-mode template when you outgrow a single instance. Hetzner is the other name to know here; comparable boxes run 5-6 dollars after their mid-2026 price bump, minus the one-click template.

Where it breaks: the fine print on all "unlimited" VPS talk is that you are the ops team. When n8n ships a breaking update, when the disk fills with execution logs, when the container falls over at 2 a.m. before your morning workflows fire -- that's your Saturday. It's genuinely not much work (I spend maybe 20 minutes a month), but it isn't zero, and Hostinger's support covers the VPS, not your workflows. Also note the upfront billing: the advertised monthly rate is the total plan price divided by the term, so the $6.49 figure means paying a year or two ahead.

2. n8n Cloud -- pay to never think about it

Starter at 20 €/month billed annually (24 € month-to-month) covers 2,500 executions, 5 concurrent runs, unlimited users, and 2,300 AI Assistant credits. Pro at 50 €/month annual raises that to 10,000 executions, 20 concurrent, workflow history, and admin roles. The thing n8n's pricing gets right -- and the reason it beats Zapier/Make economics at scale -- is that you pay per workflow run, not per step; a 40-node monster counts the same as a 2-node ping. I did the full cost math against self-hosting in n8n Cloud vs Self-Hosted.

Where it breaks: 2,500 executions sounds like a lot until you schedule something every 15 minutes -- that single cron eats 2,880 runs a month by itself. High-frequency polling workflows push you to Pro fast, and at 50 €/month you could rent eight KVM 1 boxes. Cloud is the right call when the person paying is not the person tinkering.

3. Managed n8n (Elestio and friends) -- the middle path

Elestio runs a fully managed n8n from $11/month: they handle installation, SSL, automated backups, monitoring, and OS/software updates, on your choice of underlying cloud (Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Vultr, and others), billed hourly with no commitment. You get self-hosting's unlimited executions without owning the maintenance. Similar offerings exist across the managed-app platforms, generally in the $9-19 range depending on the underlying instance size.

Where it breaks: you're paying a roughly 2x markup over the raw server for the management layer, and support covers the platform, not your workflow logic. It's the right pick if you tried the VPS route once, hated the maintenance, but still refuse execution caps on principle. That's a bigger crowd than either camp admits.

4. The free option -- hardware you already own

The honest free answer isn't a hosting company; it's the old PC in your closet. n8n's Community Edition runs happily in Docker on anything -- a Raspberry Pi handles a surprising load. Free-tier cloud tricks (Railway, Render, Oracle's free VM) either expired, sleep your instance between requests -- which silently kills scheduled triggers -- or take more setup than they're worth. A home box costs pennies in electricity and is perfect for learning and dev/test before you point real business workflows at it; the catch is exposing webhooks through your home network (a tunnel or reverse proxy) and accepting that a power flicker is your outage. FAQ

What is the best way to host n8n in 2026?

A cheap VPS with a one-click n8n template for most solo builders -- Hostinger KVM 1 at $6.49/month runs unlimited workflows and executions. n8n Cloud (20 euros/month annual) is the pick when nobody wants to touch a server.

Is n8n Cloud worth it compared to self-hosting?

At high execution volume, no -- a $6.49 VPS has no ceiling while Cloud Starter caps at 2,500 runs. At low volume where reliability matters more than price, yes: updates, backups, and uptime become n8n's problem instead of yours.

Can I host n8n for free?

The software is free; reliable free hosting mostly isn't anymore. Free cloud tiers sleep or expired, which breaks scheduled workflows. A home PC or Raspberry Pi running Docker is the one genuinely free setup that works.
