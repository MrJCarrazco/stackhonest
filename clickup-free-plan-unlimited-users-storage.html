ClickUp Free Plan: What "Unlimited Members" Actually Means (2026)

Last updated October 9, 2026 · ~10 min read · by StackHonest

Disclosure: the Zoho Projects link marked below sits in an affiliate slot on this site, but no Zoho affiliate code is live here yet, so that link earns exactly $0 today — it is a placeholder, not a payday. ClickUp, Notion and Trello have no affiliate relationship with this site at all, so every link to them is a plain editorial citation to the page I read the numbers off. Nothing here is sponsored, no vendor saw this before it went live, and this site has collected no affiliate revenue to date.

Short answer: ClickUp's Free Forever plan really does give you unlimited members and unlimited tasks -- confirmed on ClickUp's own pricing page and help center, and the rare case where a vendor's generous headline claim holds up exactly as advertised. The catch is storage, not people: ClickUp's own live pricing page prints a 60MB total storage cap for the whole Free workspace, shared across every member. Two independent 2026 trackers claim the cap is 100MB instead, and neither matches what this article found live on ClickUp's own page. On top of that, ClickUp's pricing page is currently running two different pricing structures at once -- old plan cards and a newer comparison table -- with two different prices for what looks like the same tier. Every figure below was read off ClickUp's, Notion's and Trello's own pages on October 9, 2026.

The help center backs up the member claim in its own words: "Free Forever Workspaces can have an unlimited number of members and guests at no charge," and on Free, "guests have full create and edit permissions, which cannot be modified" -- a genuinely unusual default (most tools lock free-tier guests to read-only). So the brief's core claim -- unlimited users -- checked out against the vendor's own page without needing a correction, the same way Bitwarden's free-tier brief held up intact earlier in this series.

THE 60MB vs 100MB DISCREPANCY

60MB is not a typo for 60GB. It is printed as "60MB" in the File Storage row of ClickUp's own comparison table, for the entire workspace, shared across every member -- not 60MB per person. That is small enough that a handful of screenshots or PDFs fills it.

Two independent third-party trackers, both apparently written in 2026, report a different number entirely:

One of the two third-party trackers is not even internally consistent: its own quick-summary table says 100MB while its prose elsewhere in the same piece repeats ClickUp's 60MB. This article is reporting 60MB as the number because it is the one stated on ClickUp's own current page, and naming the 100MB claim as an unresolved discrepancy rather than silently picking whichever number sounds more generous.

Same page, same visit, two different numbers for what is clearly meant to be the same rung of the ladder: $7 vs $8 for the entry paid tier, $12 vs $17 for Business. ClickUp's own page explains why, in a single caption line printed directly above the comparison table: "The comparison below shows default values of our newest plans. Existing customers who migrate to newer plans may have higher limits." Read plainly, that means ClickUp is mid-migration from an older Unlimited/Business structure to a newer Core/Business/Max structure, and the marketing cards at the top of the page have not been reconciled with the comparison table underneath them. A third-party licensing summary citing ClickUp's own help articles independently corroborates the transition: it names a "Business Plus" tier that "is not included on our pricing page" at all, plus a "Max" plan "for teams that rely heavily on AI," confirming this is a real, documented rename in progress rather than a one-off rendering glitch caught by accident.

What this means practically: which price you are actually quoted may depend on whether you are a new signup or an existing customer being migrated, and this article is not guessing which one applies to a given reader. Screenshot whatever the page shows you at checkout before you commit to annual billing.

Automation limits and the new AI-credit layer

Free Forever includes 100 Automation Actions per workspace per month in the newer comparison table (the older marketing copy for the Unlimited-era structure listed "5 Active Rules, 100 Automation executions" instead -- another small mismatch between the two structures described above). The newer table also folds in an AI allowance directly on Free: 500 AI Credits per workspace, shared, auto-top-up optional at $10 per 10,000 credits for any plan. ClickUp Brain (the AI assistant) usage beyond the free credit pool is "Limits apply" on every tier including paid ones below Max, so "AI included" does not mean unmetered AI on any plan currently listed.

None of the three is unlimited across the board. ClickUp wins outright on headcount (genuinely no cap, and the only one of the three where that claim held up without a footnote). Notion's catch only bites teams -- a true solo user never sees the 1,000-block ceiling at all, so the common "Notion free caps you at 1,000 blocks" claim repeated across many roundups is stale: it is conditional on a second member joining the workspace, not universal. Trello's board cap is the one that surprises people fastest, since ten boards arrives quickly once a team splits work by client or quarter, though archiving finished boards is a genuine (not a trick) workaround that keeps a project-shaped team under the limit indefinitely.

When ClickUp Free is the right call, and when it is not

  1. You want to add your whole team at zero cost. ClickUp is the only one of the three PM tools here with a genuinely uncapped member count on free -- confirmed, not a marketing exaggeration.
  2. You attach files, screenshots or designs regularly. 60MB total is tiny. A handful of screenshots fills it; link files from Google Drive/Dropbox instead of uploading, or budget for the Unlimited/Core tier specifically for storage.
  3. You are about to commit to annual billing. Screenshot the exact price shown to you first -- this article found ClickUp's own live page quoting two different numbers for what looks like the same tier, on the same visit.
  4. You want heavy AI usage bundled into a PM tool. 500 credits/workspace on Free will not go far for a team; check the per-tier credit allowance before assuming "AI included" means unmetered.
  5. You are a solo user who mostly needs docs and databases, not task management. Notion Free has no block cap at all for a single-member workspace -- a simpler fit than ClickUp's broader feature set for that specific use case.

Verdict

ClickUp's free-plan headline claim -- unlimited members, unlimited tasks -- is accurate and was the rare brief in this series that needed no correction. The real constraint is a 60MB total storage cap that two independent trackers get wrong (reporting 100MB with no cited source), and a pricing page that is currently serving two different price structures for what appears to be the same paid tier, with its own caption admitting a mid-migration state rather than hiding it. None of that makes ClickUp a bad tool -- unlimited free seats is genuinely rare in this category -- but it does mean the number you are quoted at checkout is worth screenshotting before you commit to annual billing, and the number you read in a third-party roundup for free-tier storage is worth re-checking against ClickUp's own page directly.

FAQ

Does ClickUp's free plan really have unlimited members?

Yes, confirmed on clickup.com/pricing's own Free Forever card: "Unlimited Free Plan Members," and the help center states "Free Forever Workspaces can have an unlimited number of members and guests at no charge." Tasks are also unlimited. The free plan is genuinely generous on headcount -- the catch is storage, not people.

How much free storage does ClickUp actually give you?

ClickUp's own live pricing page prints 60MB total, shared across the whole workspace, in its File Storage row for the Free plan -- not 60GB. Two independent third-party trackers report the cap as 100MB instead, and neither cites a source for the higher number; this article could not reproduce it on ClickUp's own page, so 60MB is reported as the vendor-stated figure and 100MB is flagged as unverified.

Why does ClickUp's own pricing page show two different sets of prices?

The plan cards near the top list Free Forever / Unlimited ($7/mo yearly, $10 monthly) / Business ($12/mo yearly, $19 monthly). Further down, a comparison table lists Free / Core ($8/user/mo) / Business ($17/user/mo) / Enterprise / Max. The page itself explains why in one caption line: "The comparison below shows default values of our newest plans. Existing customers who migrate to newer plans may have higher limits." ClickUp is mid-transition between an old and new plan structure, and both are live on the same page at once.

What are ClickUp's real paid-plan limits versus Notion and Trello free?

ClickUp Free: unlimited members and tasks, 60MB storage, 5 Spaces. Notion Free: unlimited blocks solo, 1,000-block cap once a second member joins, 5MB file uploads, 10 guests. Trello Free: unlimited cards, but 10 boards and 10 collaborators per Workspace, 10MB file attachments. Each tool is generous on one axis and capped on another.

Is there a cheaper or free alternative to ClickUp for a small team?

Zoho Projects has a genuine free tier and published per-seat pricing (Premium $4/user/month, Enterprise $9, Ultimate $14, billed annually) -- but its own free-plan user/project count is inconsistent across Zoho's own pages: 5 users/3 projects on the pricing page and a help FAQ, 3 users/2 projects on a marketing page, and 3 users/1 project in another help article. All three are reported since all three are genuinely published.
